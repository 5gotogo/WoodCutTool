ASSEMBLY CHECK / FIELD GUIDE

Dry-Fit Checks Before Fixing a Cabinet or Frame

Dry fitting is the point where dimensions, joints and hardware can be inspected together. Use this workflow to carry a measured diagonal comparison into a documented review before the next fixing operation.

By WoodCutTool editorial team · Updated 2026-10-09

STEP 01

Start with the approved parts and references

Compare the parts with the current drawing and cut list before assembling. Confirm the intended reference face, inside or outside dimensions, part orientation and labels. Saw blanks from the Edge Banding Planner and finished outside sizes serve different stages; choose the dimension that describes the assembly you are actually measuring. Keep material thickness, edging and joinery assumptions visible. If a panel is wrong, correcting the part list is more useful than forcing the assembly toward a convenient diagonal reading. Cabinet Studio can help review the intended design, while this tool records physical measurements taken at the bench. Neither tool replaces a check of the actual hardware and stock.

STEP 02

Confirm seating and opposite sides

Bring the joints into their intended dry-fit position and inspect for chips, trapped debris or an incorrect shoulder. Measure opposite widths and opposite heights on the same face. Compare actual overall dimensions with the approved values, and use a square where accessible to check the relevant corners. The first physical checkbox represents that inspection, not a conclusion derived from the two diagonal numbers. If the top and bottom widths differ, equal diagonals can hide taper. If a joint has not seated, a reading under temporary pressure may not describe the intended final condition. Leave the check open until the side and corner evidence is consistent.

STEP 03

Check support and accessible faces

Place the assembly on a suitable stable support and verify that its reference face lies in the intended plane. Inspect for rocking and compare accessible opposite faces. A back panel or temporary brace may change the geometry, so follow the construction method and inspect again after it is added. A flush-looking front does not demonstrate that the rear or the body is free of twist. Do not use a numerical pass to excuse a visible support or alignment problem. The plane checkbox records a physical review. The calculator reports no structural adequacy, installation level, anchoring requirement or service-load assessment.

STEP 04

Review both diagonals with the chosen limit

Take two repeatable readings from equivalent labeled corners. Enter the project-specific diagonal-difference tolerance and the uncertainty for each reading. The result compares the full possible difference range with that limit. A borderline outcome requires better measurement evidence or geometry review before you mark the review complete. An outside outcome tells you that the current reading range exceeds the limit, without diagnosing the cause. Recheck references before attempting an adjustment. Make any adjustment according to the construction and tool instructions, then save another reading. More clamp pressure is not a measurement method, and the tool does not prescribe clamp force or a correction distance.

STEP 05

Trial the downstream fit

Test the component that the next operation depends on. A drawer needs the relevant slide clearances and a practical trial fit; a cabinet opening may need its door, shelf or back checked; a frame may need its insert or corner joints inspected. The fourth checkbox records that fit inspection and seating check. Equal diagonals cannot certify a hinge location, a reveal dimension or a clearance. The complete-review state appears only when the reading range is within your chosen limit and all four physical checks are confirmed. It means the entered review is complete for that face and stage. A later change to geometry, support or clamps requires another physical check.

STEP 06

Save the stage and repeat after fixing

Use a descriptive name such as “Drawer 2 · dry fit · inside opening” and save the snapshot. Repeat after a relevant fixing operation or clamp change, then save a separate record with the new stage. The time-stamped history makes it easier to compare the two conditions without overwriting the first reading. Export CSV for a readable record, JSON for an editable backup, or print the current draft and history. Data stays in this browser; clearing storage can remove it, so retain a separate backup when the record matters. If storage is unavailable, the tool keeps working in the current tab and reports that exports are needed. A documented measurement should travel with its limits and references, not become an unexplained green label.
