Pros and cons

Tradeoffs by option.

Melamine pros

  • Best fit for closet systems, white interiors, utility cabinets, and low-finish labor
  • Finish: Factory finished surface
  • Edges: Requires banding and chipout control
  • Weight: Often heavy
  • Clear choice when the project is designed around Melamine.

Melamine cons

  • Can be the wrong choice when the job is closer to shop cabinets, high-use boxes, exposed edges, and moisture-prone spaces.
  • Requires checking real stock, tool setup, installation conditions, and finish expectations.
  • May cost more in rework if chosen only because Melamine is familiar.
  • Compare against Plywood with the actual measurements before buying.

Plywood pros

  • Best fit for shop cabinets, high-use boxes, exposed edges, and moisture-prone spaces
  • Finish: Needs finish or prefinished sheet
  • Edges: Stronger and easier to repair
  • Weight: Usually lighter
  • Clear choice when the project is designed around Plywood.

Plywood cons

  • Can be the wrong choice when the job is closer to closet systems, white interiors, utility cabinets, and low-finish labor.
  • Requires checking real stock, tool setup, installation conditions, and finish expectations.
  • May cost more in rework if chosen only because Plywood is familiar.
  • Compare against Melamine with the actual measurements before buying.

Maintenance

Maintenance and long-term fit

Do not compare only sticker price or the first setup step. Compare the whole workflow: measuring, buying, cutting, installing, finishing, revising, maintaining, and repairing the result. A cheaper or faster option can still lose if it creates more waste, harder cuts, weaker fastening, worse appearance, or more rework after the first mistake.
