Fastener system

Choose the joint around the panel and drilling process

Pocket screws approach the joint at an angle from a drilled pocket. The method is fast and accessible with a jig, but the pocket needs a hidden or acceptable face, the work must be clamped against shift, and screw length must suit the real stock thickness. Edge quality, plywood voids, pilot geometry, and over-driving can all change holding performance, so a test joint in project offcut is more useful than a generic screw rule.

Confirmat screws approach a panel edge in a straight, deliberately predrilled joint. They are commonly associated with repeatable casework in particleboard, MDF, and other engineered panels, where the specified pilot and shank geometry matters. They do not turn inaccurate drilling into accurate cabinetry: the panels still need square edges and controlled hole location. Use the exact fastener manufacturer's drilling guidance, verify edge distance, and test the complete joint before releasing a cabinet batch.

Release checklist

  • Identify the actual panel core and finished thickness.
  • Keep the drilling face and fastener head visible in the plan.
  • Use the matched jig, bit, pilot, and screw specification.
  • Build and inspect a test corner before production assembly.

Pros and cons

Tradeoffs by option.

Pocket screws pros

  • Best fit for face frames, plywood cabinets, hidden interior fastening, shop fixtures, and builds already using a pocket-hole jig
  • Fastener path: Angled through a pocket
  • Drilling: Pocket-hole jig and matched bit
  • Best material fit: Plywood and solid-wood assemblies
  • Clear choice when the project is designed around Pocket screws.

Pocket screws cons

  • Can be the wrong choice when the job is closer to particleboard or MDF casework, straight panel-to-edge joints, knock-down access, and repeatable predrilled production.
  • Requires checking real stock, tool setup, installation conditions, and finish expectations.
  • May cost more in rework if chosen only because Pocket screws is familiar.
  • Compare against Confirmat screws with the actual measurements before buying.

Confirmat screws pros

Maintenance

Maintenance and long-term fit

Do not compare only sticker price or the first setup step. Compare the whole workflow: measuring, buying, cutting, installing, finishing, revising, maintaining, and repairing the result. A cheaper or faster option can still lose if it creates more waste, harder cuts, weaker fastening, worse appearance, or more rework after the first mistake.
