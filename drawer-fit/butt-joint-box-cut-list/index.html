DRAWER FIT STUDIO / FIELD GUIDE 03

A Drawer Box Cut List with Explicit Butt-Joint Geometry

A cut list is reliable only when its construction rule is visible. This planner uses full-length sides, front and back between the sides, and a bottom applied underneath.

By WoodCutTool editorial team · Reviewed 2026-10-23

Open your drawer plan ↗

WORKED REVIEW

A 474 × 500 × 150 mm outside box with 12 mm walls and a 6 mm applied bottom has two 500 × 144 mm sides, two 450 × 144 mm front/back parts and one 500 × 474 mm bottom. Three drawers multiply quantities to 6, 6 and 3.

1. Define the outside envelope2. Subtract only the joints this model contains3. Use measured stock and a physical assembly4. Export finished parts, then plan the cuts

STEP 01

Define the outside envelope

Outside box width comes from the mounting-space calculation. Outside length is a deliberate input checked against setback and rear reserve; it is not automatically equal to slide length. Total outside height includes the applied bottom. Keep decorative front thickness separate from the structural front of the box. This convention allows a measured cabinet corridor and the construction rule to produce a reproducible parts schedule. A slide can impose additional box-length or height requirements that the envelope alone cannot prove.

STEP 02

Subtract only the joints this model contains

The sides run the complete outside length. The structural front and back fit between them, so their length is outside width minus twice the actual wall thickness. Every wall part has height equal to total outside box height minus applied-bottom thickness. The bottom covers the complete box footprint. These formulas do not include rabbets, locking joints, dovetail tails, groove engagement or an inset back. If the joinery changes, this cut list no longer represents the construction and must be recalculated with the correct method.

STEP 03

Use measured stock and a physical assembly

Measure representative wall and bottom stock before generating the final revision. A nominal sheet label is not an actual thickness measurement. Dry-assemble one box and compare its outside width, length and diagonals with the plan. Select glue, fasteners, edge distances and bottom attachment for the materials and intended use; the geometry model does not design those connections or assign a load capacity. Verify that screw heads and the applied bottom do not interfere with the selected runner or locking mechanism.

STEP 04

Export finished parts, then plan the cuts

The CSV contains four part families, each with quantity, length, width and thickness. They are finished component dimensions. Do not add blade kerf to every part size; kerf belongs in the stock layout and cutting sequence. Decide grain direction, material grouping, edging and trim before transferring the schedule to sheet planning. Keep the decorative fronts in their own thickness and material group. Record the project revision on labels so a changed opening does not leave an older stack of parts mixed into the batch.

Before continuing

Change the construction method before using these numbers for grooved bottoms, dovetails, metal-sided drawers or an undermount system.

Does the cut list include blade kerf?

No. It exports finished part sizes. Add kerf, stock trimming and grain constraints when arranging those parts on boards or sheets.
