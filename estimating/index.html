The estimating desk reference

Eight checks between a measurement and an order

Use these checks alongside every estimate. Open a focused existing tool when the job needs more detail.

Check 01

Keep dimensions and prices in the same unit

Record dimensions in either millimeters or inches for the entire calculation. Switching units here converts the physical dimensions, including sheet size; it does not change the price per sheet. For solid lumber sold by volume, use the board-foot calculator instead of treating a board as sheet stock.

Board-foot calculator →

Check 02

Separate finished sizes from billed stock

A supplier bills for the stock you buy, while the part dimensions describe what you need after processing. Check actual sheet size, usable edges, thickness and surface specification. Compare prices only after matching those details. A cheaper sheet with the wrong thickness or finish is not an equivalent quote.

Compare sheet materials →

Check 03

Name the allowance before using a percentage

This estimator adds a purchasing allowance to net panel area: net area × (1 + allowance / 100). It is not a measured cutting-waste percentage or a guaranteed yield. A 10% allowance on 10 square meters gives 11 square meters; a 90% yield target would require 10 / 0.9 = 11.11 square meters.

Check waste calculations →

Check 04

Round each stock group independently

Divide allowed area by sheet area and round up to whole sheets. Repeat separately for each material, thickness and finish. Do not combine a thin back with thick carcass panels to remove a sheet from the order. Keep any useful stock already on hand separate until its dimensions and condition have been checked.

Check a sheet layout →

Check 05

Check part fit before trusting the total

Area is a lower-bound planning method, not a nesting algorithm. Parts can fail to pack because of their shape, grain, kerf or edge trim even when total area looks sufficient. This tool flags a single part that exceeds the stock dimensions, but only a reviewed layout can establish a practical sheet requirement.

Check saw kerf →

Check 06

Count exposed edges explicitly

Measure only the edges that will actually receive banding, then allow for end trimming and setup waste separately. Confirm whether your part dimensions describe the core panel or the finished banded part. Keep banding material and application labor outside the sheet price so neither is counted twice.

Review component dimensions →

Check 07

Keep labor and purchased materials separate

Use estimated hours multiplied by your own hourly cost for this early allowance. Include setup and handling deliberately rather than hiding them in sheet waste. The output is a partial cost subtotal, not a client quote: hardware, transport, overhead, taxes and profit may still need their own lines.
