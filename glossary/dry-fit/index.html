Measuring and layout

What is Dry Fit?

A dry fit is a temporary assembly made without final glue, permanent fasteners, or finish. It verifies that parts, joints, hardware, clearances, and the assembly sequence work before the project reaches an irreversible step.

Why Dry Fit matters

Dry fitting exposes dimension errors while parts can still be trimmed or remade. It also reveals whether clamps can reach, joints close fully, drawers and doors clear adjacent parts, and a large assembly can be built in the planned order.

How to use it in a project

Deburr and label the parts, assemble them in the real sequence, check diagonals and reference faces, test moving hardware, and mark corrections directly on the cut list. Repeat the dry fit after any correction that changes geometry. When the project has many parts or expensive material, move from a rough note to a real cut list calculator, plywood cut calculator, or saved CutList layout before buying or cutting stock.

Dry Fit project example

Before gluing a cabinet carcass, assemble the sides, top, bottom, fixed shelves, and back without glue, then compare diagonals and confirm the back seats fully in its rabbets.

Common dry fit mistake

Checking individual joints but skipping the complete assembly can hide accumulated error, inaccessible clamp positions, or a part that cannot be inserted once another joint is glued.
