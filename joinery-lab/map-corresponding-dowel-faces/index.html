Map Dowel Centers From Corresponding Faces

A coordinate has meaning only with its datum. Opposing faces need corresponding marks that continue to refer to the same place after one part turns over.

By WoodCutTool editorial team · Reviewed 2026-10-18 · 3 minute method

WORKED REVIEW / ILLUSTRATIVE INPUTS

A 300 mm face with four centers and 30 mm insets produces centers at 30, 110, 190 and 270 mm. The row lies 30 mm from the marked side on a 60 mm-wide face. Those numbers describe each labeled face frame; the drawing is not an instruction to turn an unmarked part over and measure from whichever edge is nearest.

BENCH CHECK 01

Label the mating faces in assembly position

Place the two parts in their intended relationship and mark the faces that meet. Mark corresponding ends and sides while that relationship is visible. Include orientation arrows or a simple assembly sketch where helpful. Keep decorative outside faces distinct from the boring reference faces so the parts can be returned to the same frame after handling.

BENCH CHECK 02

Define the coordinate frame in words

Write that center distance starts at the marked end and row offset starts at the marked side. The tool models two equal, opposing rectangular faces with matching local coordinates. It does not infer a jig transformation for a corner or edge-to-face joint. For another configuration, prepare its dedicated drawing and follow the jig manufacturer’s setup rather than reinterpreting these coordinates.

BENCH CHECK 03

Compare the physical reference method

Review how the actual jig registers against a face, end and side. Manufacturer systems have specific reference marks, spacers and configuration steps. Confirm that the entered offset can be produced by the chosen setup and that the marks remain visible after clamping. A coordinate table does not establish a jig’s available range or certify a particular configuration.

BENCH CHECK 04

Prove the correspondence with a test pair

Use the intended configuration on matched scraps and check whether the corresponding centers and selected exterior faces align in assembly. Record the successful orientation in the notes or a photo kept with the job. Recheck after changing a spacer, fence reference or face selection. Preserve the paired test even if every coordinate in the digital record remains unchanged.

Before repeating the setup

  • Mating faces and corresponding ends are marked.
  • The row offset uses a named physical side.
  • The actual jig configuration has passed a paired test.
Stop and resolve the cause

Stop if a turnover changes the intended datum, a jig reference is unclear, or the real joint is outside the equal-face configuration.

Can this row map replace an edge-to-face jig manual?

No. This model maps two identical opposing faces. A manufacturer’s corner or edge-to-face configuration can require different datums, spacers, offsets and depth choices.

Method boundary and references

One row on two identical, opposing faces. Coordinates start at each face’s corresponding marked datum. This is not an edge-to-face jig setup or a dowel capacity calculation. The worked numbers are an authored geometry example. They are not a manufacturer specification or a joint-capacity test. Keep the design detail, tool instructions and current physical coupon with the project record.
