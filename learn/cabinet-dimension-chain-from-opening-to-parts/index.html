Cabinet Dimension Chain: Opening to Finished Parts

A cabinet dimension chain records how a field opening becomes a cabinet envelope and how that envelope becomes individual parts. It prevents one assumption—such as nominal plywood thickness, door overlay, or back construction—from being subtracted twice or forgotten. The chain should be readable from opening to installed cabinet without relying on mental arithmetic at the saw.

Separate the field opening from the cabinet envelope

Record the finished opening at several heights and depths, then identify the limiting width, height, and depth. Keep wall irregularity, floor slope, adjacent appliances, door swing, and required service space outside the cabinet box dimensions. The field measurement checklist helps establish this boundary. A cabinet that merely fits the widest measurement can still fail at a narrow point or leave no room for leveling and scribing.

Write one equation for each axis

For width, start with the approved cabinet envelope and show how side thickness, internal partitions, fillers, and hardware clearances create clear openings. For height, separate the box, toe-kick platform, countertop, and installation gap. For depth, distinguish the box, applied door or drawer front, finished end, back treatment, and countertop projection. Label every number as field, finished, nominal, or calculated so the same allowance cannot move silently between steps.

Resolve construction before calculating parts

A captured back, applied back, full top, stretchers, face frame, frameless box, inset front, and overlay front all create different deductions. Select the construction detail before calculating side, deck, shelf, and back sizes. Use the actual thickness definition when stock measurements differ from nominal names, and keep joinery depth visible instead of hiding it in a memorized shop rule.

Test the chain with one cabinet

Choose a representative base or wall cabinet and calculate the outside box, clear opening, door or drawer-front envelope, and every carcass part. Reassemble the arithmetic in reverse: the parts and joints should return to the approved box size, and the installed box plus fillers should return to the field allocation. If the run still exceeds the wall, use the cabinet-run diagnostic before reducing random parts.

Release dimensions with their assumptions

Put the measured opening, cabinet allocation, actual stock thickness, construction type, hardware model, and revision on the released cut list. Link the calculation to a base cabinet template or another project-specific template, then test the resulting rectangles in CutList. The goal is not a universal cabinet formula; it is a traceable chain that can be reviewed when one input changes.
