Dado and Rabbet Allowance in Cut Lists

Dados and rabbets change how finished outside dimensions become individual part sizes. Their width, depth, shoulder, reference face, and mating material thickness must agree with the cabinet dimension chain. Hiding those values inside a shop habit makes revisions and hardware changes harder to audit, especially when plywood thickness differs from its nominal label.

Draw the section before calculating

Sketch the joint in cross-section and label the finished outside surfaces, mating part, actual stock thickness, groove or rabbet width, depth, and remaining shoulder. Identify which part overlaps and which part fits inside. The dado joint and rabbet joint definitions provide a starting vocabulary, but the project drawing controls the actual detail.

Keep engagement separate from part size

Calculate the finished cabinet envelope first, then show how joint engagement changes the cut length or width of the mating part. Do not subtract the joint twice—once in the cabinet formula and again at the saw. Mark whether the cut-list dimension is finished part size before machining or the visible distance after assembly.

Use actual material and a test joint

Measure the accepted plywood batch and make a representative joint with the intended tool, cutter, and reference setup. Verify width, depth, shoulder, assembly fit, and face condition. If the joint is too shallow or deep, use depth troubleshooting; if it is too wide, use the width diagnostic before modifying production parts.

Record machining references

State the reference face and edge, distance to the near shoulder, joint length, stopped or through condition, and whether left and right parts mirror. Put those notes with the part ID and revision. The machining schedule should distinguish a dado from an edge rabbet even when the same cutter or setup is used.

Recalculate after any thickness change

A material substitution can alter the joint fit, inside opening, back size, shelf length, or overall cabinet dimensions. Update the source model, build a new test joint, and regenerate affected cut lists and sheet layouts. Use the base cabinet template to keep parts explicit rather than applying an undocumented trim to several rows.
