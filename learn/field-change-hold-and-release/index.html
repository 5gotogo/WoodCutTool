Field Change Hold-and-Release Workflow

A field change becomes dangerous when it is communicated as an informal trim instruction without tracing the effect on adjacent parts, hardware, finish, material, or already-cut work. A hold-and-release workflow pauses only the affected scope, captures the real condition, evaluates options, and issues one approved revision. It preserves progress elsewhere without allowing schedule pressure to turn an unverified site observation into a permanent shop or installation decision.

Open a specific hold

Record the room, location, observed condition, date, reporter, current revision, affected cabinet or part IDs, and work that must stop. Separate the observed fact from the proposed solution. “Opening measures 18 mm narrower at finished-floor height” is evidence; “trim both fillers” is a choice that still needs dimensional and finish review.

Capture usable field evidence

Measure from defined finished references at enough points to describe taper, level, plumb, bow, obstruction, or service location. Add photos or sketches with orientation and a known scale when authorized. Identify unfinished surfaces or pending work that could change the result. Use the field-measurement method rather than forwarding an isolated number without context.

Map the change impact

Trace the condition through cabinet envelopes, fillers, reveals, finished ends, doors, drawers, hardware, countertops, utilities, attachment, transport, and adjacent rooms where shared parts exist. Inventory affected material as uncut, cut, machined, finished, packed, delivered, or installed. The change-impact guide keeps the review at project level instead of editing one row prematurely.

Approve one controlled option

Compare remeasurement, field adjustment, shop rework, remake, substitution, or design revision with their dimensional, appearance, schedule, and cost consequences. Structural, utility, appliance, and code questions must remain with the responsible qualified parties. Record the selected option, approver, constraints, and rejected alternatives so the release cannot be mistaken for general permission to improvise.

Issue the revised production package

Update drawings, part IDs, quantities, dimensions, material, layout, labels, purchase differences, and installation notes together. Mark obsolete copies and physically segregate affected old parts. If the change is not in the cut list, use the change-order diagnostic before releasing replacement material.

Close the hold at the workface

The person performing the work should receive the approved revision and acknowledge the exact hold being released. Verify the first affected part or installed condition before repeating the solution. Record final dimensions and disposition of old material, then carry the result into the as-built package and cut-list revision release. Confirm that adjacent work released earlier still matches the corrected reference; a local field fix can otherwise move the mismatch into the next cabinet or panel.
