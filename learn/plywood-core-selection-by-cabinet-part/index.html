Plywood Core Selection by Cabinet Part

Plywood core selection should follow the part's job rather than a single rule for the whole cabinet. A visible shelf edge, painted door, long cabinet side, drawer bottom, back, and shop jig can value flatness, weight, edge appearance, machining, fastener holding, finish consistency, and price differently. Supplier product data and a physical sample belong in the decision.

Classify parts before choosing material

Group parts by visibility, load, span, edge exposure, machining, hardware, finish, and replacement risk. Cabinet sides and shelves may need a different balance from backs or drawer bottoms. Keep structural requirements and manufacturer specifications outside a generic furniture comparison. The veneer-core, MDF-core, and particle-core definitions provide a common vocabulary for supplier conversations.

Inspect edge and face requirements

A clear-finished exposed edge makes internal plies and voids visible. A painted face can make surface uniformity more important. Prefinished material introduces scratch and repair constraints. Ask for the exact product construction, face grade, thickness tolerance, panel weight, and intended uses rather than assuming every sheet under the same retail label behaves alike.

Match machining and hardware

Pilot holes, dados, rabbets, hinge cups, edge fasteners, and screw threads interact with the core. Test representative machining and fasteners in an offcut from the actual product, following hardware and material guidance. If a void appears on a finished edge, use the core-void diagnostic to decide whether the part can be relocated, repaired, or must be remade.

Separate material groups in the cut list

Do not combine two core types because their nominal thickness and finish look similar in a spreadsheet. Give each purchasable product a material code, actual size, face rule, grain rule, and quantity. Run separate sheet layouts so an optimizer cannot place a back or hidden part on material reserved for visible cabinet ends.

Make a project-specific selection record

Record the supplier product, sample date, measured thickness, finish system, edge treatment, hardware tests, and accepted part groups. Link the result to the plywood grade guide and a real cabinet template. The selection is valid for that documented product and project, not every panel sold under a broad species name.
