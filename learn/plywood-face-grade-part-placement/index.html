Plywood Face Grade and Part Placement

A geometric layout can fit every rectangle and still be wrong for the material. Plywood face grade, visible-side selection, grain continuity, color, patches, defects, and finish direction determine where important parts can come from the sheet. Part placement should begin with a face map and a visibility hierarchy before yield is optimized. Preserve that map until the visible groups have been labeled and accepted, because the physical relationship is lost once the sheet is broken down.

Create a visibility hierarchy

Classify parts as primary visible, secondary visible, exposed edge, or hidden. Doors, drawer fronts, finished ends, and prominent shelves usually deserve first selection; backs, nailers, and hidden dividers may accept different face conditions when the specification allows. The face veneer and finished face definitions keep those labels consistent.

Map the actual sheet

Inspect both faces under useful light and mark patches, color changes, veneer splits, dents, core voids visible at edges, and areas that should not enter a finished part. Photograph or sketch the sheet with a reference corner. A broad grade designation does not replace inspection of the individual panel received for the project.

Place visual groups before utility parts

Reserve coherent zones for matched doors, drawer fronts, adjacent finished ends, or furniture panels whose grain should read together. Lock their orientation and identify their sequence. Then place shelves, dividers, and hidden parts around those groups. This can produce a lower numerical yield than unrestricted nesting while delivering the required visible result.

Control color and batch variation

Panels from the same product line can still differ in color or grain. Keep sheets and parts labeled by batch and cabinet location, and review adjacent visible parts before edge treatment or assembly. If panels disagree, use the color-variation diagnostic to evaluate sorting, location, finish samples, and replacement options.

Record the placement decision

The released layout should show sheet identity, reference corner, face direction, part labels, grain arrows, defect exclusions, and visual grouping. Use the rotation benchmark to understand material pressure, then use CutList to save the project-specific constraints. Do not remove a visual rule merely to recover the lowest sheet count.
