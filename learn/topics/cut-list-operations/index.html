How do I keep a cut list accurate from the first part entry through revision, production, handoff, and closeout?

This hub is a navigation and decision surface. The pillar guide explains the full method, supporting guides isolate one input or workflow, Troubleshooting diagnoses failures, Checklists release irreversible work, Worksheets preserve project evidence, and calculators or apps apply the reviewed inputs.

1

Create stable identities

Give projects, rooms, assemblies, parts, material groups, revisions, and replacement items names that remain unique.

2

Release complete inputs

Verify finished dimensions, quantities, actual material, allowances, grain, rotation, hardware, and every required component.

3

Control production changes

Map each revision to affected parts, stock, layouts, orders, labels, already-cut work, holds, and replacement decisions.

4

Reconcile closeout

Confirm accepted final quantities, installed or delivered state, remakes, surplus, reusable offcuts, superseded files, and as-built records.

Evidence required

Make the result reproducible

A durable cut-list package includes the released revision, complete part and material inputs, layout, labels, change log, affected-part review, purchase bridge, status records, remakes, and accepted closeout.

Stop condition

Do not turn uncertainty into permission

Stop when part IDs collide, dimensions or units are ambiguous, material groups are missing, the current revision is unclear, or a change has not been traced to existing work and orders.

Questions before release

Cut List Operations FAQ

What makes a cut-list revision controlled?

It names the changed inputs, affected parts and material, already-cut work, layouts, orders, labels, approver, and new release.

Should replacement parts reuse the old part ID?

Keep the relationship visible, but distinguish the replacement and its revision so rejected and accepted pieces cannot be confused.

When is a cut list closed?

When final accepted quantities, installed or delivered status, remakes, surplus, offcuts, purchasing differences, and as-built records agree.
