Freeze the finished design

Record finished width and length, block and row counts, borders, orientation, and the role of every fabric.

Translate finished to cut dimensions

Apply the pattern's seam allowances, subcutting method, directional constraints, and repeat matching before totaling pieces.

3

Convert pieces to yardage

Use real usable fabric width, efficient strip or shape grouping, backing seams, binding joins, and whole-unit purchasing.

4

Preserve the calculation

Save the pattern version, inputs, fabric assignments, yardage result, substitutions, and remaining fabric for later repair or repetition.

Evidence required

Make the result reproducible

Retain the finished design, pattern version, cut-size assumptions, usable width, fabric roles, directional rules, piece count, backing and binding method, calculated yardage, purchase rounding, and saved revision.

Stop condition

Do not turn uncertainty into permission

Stop when finished and cut dimensions are mixed, seam allowance is unclear, directional fabric changes the layout, or a substitution changes usable width or repeat.

Quilt Planning FAQ

Why separate finished and cut dimensions?

Seam allowances and subcutting change the fabric required; mixing the two produces systematic shortages or oversize pieces.

Does directional fabric require more yardage?

Often yes, because pieces may not rotate freely and pattern repeats may need matching.

What should be saved with the yardage result?

Keep the pattern version, finished size, piece counts, usable width, fabric roles, assumptions, and purchase rounding.
