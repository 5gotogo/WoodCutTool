How do I make materials, documents, machines, parts, and offcuts move through the shop without losing identity?

This hub is a navigation and decision surface. The pillar guide explains the full method, supporting guides isolate one input or workflow, Troubleshooting diagnoses failures, Checklists release irreversible work, Worksheets preserve project evidence, and calculators or apps apply the reviewed inputs.

1

Map movement and hazards

Record delivery paths, stock handling, machine envelopes, infeed and outfeed, support, dust control, exits, and temporary staging.

2

Create stable identities

Use material, project, room, part, revision, batch, and status labels that remain with physical items and documents.

3

Separate work states

Keep received, accepted, staged, cut, inspected, held, rejected, remake, finished, packed, and closed items visibly distinct.

4

Close the loop

Reconcile remaining parts, remakes, reusable offcuts, scrap, files, maintenance, and lessons before the project leaves the shop.

Evidence required

Make the result reproducible

A reliable shop record includes storage and machine zones, accepted stock, current work package, part and batch labels, hold area, offcut inventory, remake disposition, maintenance status, and closeout ownership.

Stop condition

Do not turn uncertainty into permission

Stop when stock cannot be handled safely, current and obsolete revisions are mixed, held parts can re-enter production, or an offcut cannot be identified and verified before reuse.

Apply the method

Move from reading to a checked project action

Choose the surface matching the next decision. Calculators and apps test inputs, Worksheets retain project values, Checklists control release, Troubleshooting isolates disagreements, and source-linked examples or research make assumptions inspectable.

Questions before release

Shop Workflow FAQ

What is the minimum useful shop labeling system?

At minimum, preserve project, part or material identity, revision, quantity or size, status, and destination.

When does an offcut become inventory?

When another person can find it, identify its material and usable size, confirm its condition, and reserve it reliably.

Why separate held and rejected work?

A hold may return after a decision; rejected work needs a documented disposition and must not silently mix with accepted production.

Cluster maintenance rule

Improve the existing route before creating a close variant
