Measure Artwork, Mat and Backing for a Wood Frame

Record the display crop and the actual package as separate measurements before choosing a frame profile.

By WoodCutTool editorial team · 2026-11-20

Decide what will be visible Measure the largest outer layer Check orientation and mat borders Keep one dimension record Bench check

Decide what will be visible

Lay the print flat and note its full paper size, including any white border or signature that matters. Next, mark the intended visible image with loose paper strips. The mat window sets that crop. Its outside dimensions set the size of the package that goes into the frame. A print sold as 8 × 10 inches may have margins you want to keep, so check the actual piece before specifying the window.

Measure the largest outer layer

Stack the glazing, mat or spacer, artwork and backing in their intended order. Measure the widest and tallest layer, even if they are different layers. Check at both ends of each axis and compare diagonals for any rigid sheet that looks skewed. A single nominal size written on the packaging will miss a proud edge or out-of-square corner.

Check orientation and mat borders

Mark top on the reverse and sketch the planned orientation. For a bottom-weighted mat, record the four borders individually and compare the visible border after the moulding lip covers the perimeter. On a no-mat project, use an overlay of the calculated sight rectangle to locate any covered signature or lettering.

Keep one dimension record

Write the actual package width and height with units, window size, top edge and maximum stack thickness. Leave clearance out of the package dimensions. The planner adds it when sizing the rabbet pocket.

Bench check

Trace the assembled package on scrap paper and lay a paper sight opening over it. Confirm the same crop and fit in the intended hanging orientation.
