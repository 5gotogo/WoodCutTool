The planning promise

Know the next decision before material, time, or site access is committed

Finish with a movable or permitted restrained bench that drains, dries, opens safely, and never consumes the balcony's required access or outlet path.

This playbook turns that promise into a controlled sequence: capture the project brief, verify material and hardware groups, work through three consequential choices, complete six observable phases, and close against a named finish line. It is designed to keep related resources together without pretending that a generic page knows the dimensions, loads, products, tools, site conditions, or requirements of a real job.

This is a planning playbook, not a dimensioned construction drawing, engineering review, code approval, machine instruction, or manufacturer authorization. Use it to organize questions and evidence. The released design, current product data, qualified review, field measurements, test pieces, and applicable requirements remain authoritative.

Private local working copy

Write the project brief before choosing a path

A useful brief records both the value and its source. Write the room, product, drawing, measurement method, responsible person, date, and revision where those details control the result. Entries remain in this browser when storage is available; they are not uploaded by this page.

Do not use a remembered dimension as a released input. If the source is missing, mark the field open and keep dependent purchasing, cutting, drilling, finishing, or installation on hold.

Choose, then reveal

Three decisions that change the project downstream

Select the condition that matches the verified brief before opening the consequence. This small pause prevents a familiar preference from masquerading as a project fact. The reveal explains what must be carried into dimensions, material, sequence, and evidence; it does not approve the choice.

Decision challenge 1 of 3

Should the storage compartment be watertight?

A sealed box can still receive condensation and small leaks, then hold water against stored items and joints.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathShed bulk water while providing drainage and cross-ventilation: Water has a visible route out and contents can dry. Outdoor storage should manage water, not promise an impossible seal.

Conditional — verifyUse removable weather-resistant bins inside a ventilated bench: The bench structure and stored contents have separate protection layers. Keep bins removable without blocking drainage.

Stop and resolveCaulk every joint into an airtight plywood chest: Trapped moisture can remain unseen and damage the panel core. Do not eliminate the drying path.

Decision challenge 2 of 3

How will the lid behave in wind?

An open outdoor lid can act as a sail, while a loose closed lid can lift or slam.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathUse corrosion-compatible positive open and closed restraints: Both lid states are deliberate and tested at the final weight. Control wind before adding cushions or hands near the hinge.

Conditional — verifyUse a removable lightweight panel stored indoors: This can suit infrequent dry storage when handling and edge wear are acceptable. Provide a safe storage location for the removed panel.

Stop and resolveRely on lid weight: Gusts and leverage can overcome weight unpredictably. Mass is not a wind latch.

Decision challenge 3 of 3

Can the bench attach to the building?

Railings, waterproof membranes, facades, and shared structures may prohibit drilling or added wind loads.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathFollow written property requirements and use an approved method: The installation boundary is explicit and responsibility is clear. Permission is a design input, not a closeout detail.

Conditional — verifyKeep the bench freestanding within a tested low stable footprint: This can comply when rules allow and movement, wind, and path tests pass. Do not create hidden attachment or railing loads.

Stop and resolveDrill into railing, facade, or deck membrane without approval: The work may damage waterproofing or regulated building systems. Stop and obtain property or qualified review.

After each reveal, record the verified condition in the brief and update every dependent dimension, material row, phase, and release check before continuing.

Six-phase path

Move from verified input to documented finish

Complete phases in the order shown unless the released project plan defines a safer dependency. Each checkbox represents a reviewed phase, not merely activity. Preserve the input, observed result, owner, date, revision, and exception record that lets another person reproduce why the work moved forward.

01

Phase 1 of 6 · 60–90 min

Measure

Outcome

Document permission, balcony path, drainage, exposure, delivery, and lid movement.

Work sequence

  • Verify building and lease boundaries.
  • Map door, drain, slope, railing, and outlet.
  • Measure the complete delivery route and wind-exposed lid arc.
Evidence to keep

Approved boundary note and balcony/delivery survey.

Hold point

Stop if attachment, load, fire, egress, or drainage requirements are unresolved.

02

Phase 2 of 6 · 2–3 hr

Plan

Outcome

Create a modular, draining, ventilated bench with controlled lid and replaceable weather details.

Work sequence

  • Split modules for the delivery path.
  • Design water shedding and ventilation.
  • Choose stable base, lid restraints, and allowed placement.
Evidence to keep

Exploded bench and water-path drawing.

Hold point

Stop if any part blocks a drain, railing function, door, or required access path.

03

Phase 3 of 6 · 3–5 hr plus finish

Cut

Outcome

Prepare exterior parts, protected edges, and assembly interfaces before weather exposure.

Work sequence

  • Cut modules and drainage openings.
  • Ease and seal every specified edge.
  • Mock up hinge, latch, foot, and liner details.
Evidence to keep

Coated edge sample and passing lid/drainage mockup.

Hold point

Stop on exposed incompatible core, trapped horizontal ledges, or hardware corrosion conflict.

04

Phase 4 of 6 · 3–5 hr plus cure

Assemble

Outcome

Build square transportable modules and verify lid, ventilation, and drainage before delivery.

Work sequence

  • Assemble modules with serviceable fasteners.
  • Install lid controls and removable liner or floor.
  • Run a controlled hose or poured-water path test.
Evidence to keep

Dry interior after test with visible drainage and controlled lid cycles.

Hold point

Stop if water pools, enters unprotected core, or cannot be inspected and removed.

05

Phase 5 of 6 · 60–120 min

Install

Outcome

Carry, connect, level, and place the bench within approved balcony boundaries.

Work sequence

  • Move modules through the verified route.
  • Set non-staining feet clear of drainage.
  • Connect modules and apply only approved restraint.
Evidence to keep

Final path, drain, foot, and restraint clearance record.

Hold point

Stop if the bench rocks toward the railing or reduces the required path.

06

Phase 6 of 6 · 45–75 min

Closeout

Outcome

Test seat load, wind-control hardware, drainage, cleaning, and seasonal storage routine.

Work sequence

  • Apply the declared seating load.
  • Cycle and latch the lid in both states.
  • Repeat drainage test and document inspection intervals.
Evidence to keep

Use and weather-maintenance record.

Hold point

Do not close while the lid can slam, water remains trapped, or the bench migrates under hand load.

Adapt without losing control

Useful variations and what they force you to revisit

A variation is not a cosmetic label. It can change the finished envelope, part set, sheet grouping, joinery, hardware, load, stability, transport, finish, installation, or removal path. Choose it early enough to update the authoritative project sources.

Two-module carry-in bench

Use when: Use when elevator, stair, or balcony door limits reject the complete finished bench but permit two safe lifting modules.

Carry through: A serviceable connector seam and exterior assembly sequence replace one rigid box while seat load must cross the joint.

Slatted bench with removable bins

Use when: Use when approved weather-resistant bins can protect contents and leave the wood shell open to drain and dry.

Carry through: Water management simplifies, while bin handles, lift clearance, wind retention, and replacement size control storage.

Low cushion platform

Use when: Use when dry cushions can be stored elsewhere and the balcony needs seating more than enclosed volume.

Carry through: Lid risks disappear and wind profile drops, but cushion retention, drying, and open slat support remain necessary.

Catch rework while it is still a decision

Project risks, early signals, and hold actions

Use these risks as observation prompts rather than predictions. When a signal appears, protect the current condition, keep affected work visible, identify the smallest upstream cause, and repeat every dependent calculation, sample, fit, or inspection under the corrected revision.

Unauthorized building attachment

Early signal: The plan requires drilling railing, facade, slab, membrane, or shared structure without written method approval.

Response: Stop installation and obtain property or qualified direction, or redesign as an allowed tested freestanding unit.

Blocked drain or exit

Early signal: The final feet, open lid, seated user, or stored module overlaps the surveyed drain or required access path.

Response: Move or resize the bench and repeat simultaneous path and drainage checks before loading or restraint.

Wind-driven lid

Early signal: The lid lifts against its latch, accelerates near open, or moves the bench during a controlled hand check.

Response: Keep the lid closed and unload it until positive open/closed restraint and final-weight geometry are corrected.

Condensation trap

Early signal: Moisture remains on liner, floor, underside, or panel edges after the weather test and drying interval.

Response: Remove contents and liner, open the drainage and ventilation path, and repair affected finish before reuse.

Never solve a mismatch by trimming or forcing the downstream part without recording what changed. A local adjustment can hide a wrong datum, wrong product, wrong material thickness, unsafe clearance, blocked assembly sequence, or installation conflict that will reappear later.

Finish line

Close the project against observable results

The balcony bench respects the building before it serves storage: it travels in, stays within permission, preserves drainage and access, and controls outdoor water and lid motion.

  • Each finished module passes the recorded elevator, stair, door, turn, and lifting route without impact or uncontrolled handling.
  • The installed bench, open lid, and seated user remain outside the required access path, drain, railing, and door envelopes.
  • A controlled water test exits through the designed path and leaves no hidden standing water after the inspection interval.
  • The finished lid stays positively held open and closed while the bench remains stable under its declared seat load.

Closeout should leave a usable trail for repair, repeat work, disassembly, warranty questions, and future changes. Save the released drawing or sketch, final parts and material records, product identities, approved exceptions, field changes, finish information, photographs where appropriate, and the final project-playbook CSV together.

Safety and authority boundaries

Use the playbook to expose uncertainty, not to override it

Follow current manufacturer instructions and use appropriate training, guarding, support, dust or fume control, ventilation, PPE, lifting methods, cure time, and disposal practices. Do not use this page as permission to operate unfamiliar equipment, defeat a safety system, exceed a tool or product rating, improvise a structural connection, or conceal work that has not passed the required inspection.

Structural, stair, guard, anchoring, seismic, electrical, plumbing, gas, fire, accessibility, and building-code decisions require the appropriate qualified professional or authority. Hardware clearances, fastener schedules, adhesive preparation, coating compatibility, load ratings, and installation methods come from the exact current product and project documentation. Verify them after any substitution.

Dimensions must name their datum, unit, finished or unfinished state, and measurement source. Check real openings at multiple locations; confirm square, level, plumb, flatness, obstructions, access, transport, assembly, service, and removal paths. Build a first article, sample, mockup, or dry fit when one failure could repeat across a batch or become expensive after finishing or installation.

Questions before starting

Project playbook FAQ

Can I build the balcony storage bench project playbook exactly as shown?

No. This page is a planning playbook rather than a dimensioned drawing or approval. Verify the real site, design, loads, material, hardware, joinery, tooling, finish, installation, and applicable requirements before irreversible work.

Where is my project progress saved?

When browser storage is available, progress stays locally in this browser under a versioned WoodCutTool record. This page does not require an account and does not send the brief, choices, or phase status to a server.

What should I do when a phase cannot be completed?

Keep the affected work on hold. Identify whether the mismatch comes from the design, measurement, material, hardware, setup, site, sequence, or current instructions. Correct the controlling source and repeat dependent checks before continuing.

Which CSV should I download?

The static project-playbook.csv is a blank durable record of the published brief, materials, decisions, and six phases. The progress CSV is created in your browser and includes the values and completion state currently entered on the page.
