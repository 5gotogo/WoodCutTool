The planning promise

Know the next decision before material, time, or site access is committed

Finish with a vanity that accepts the actual plumbing and top, keeps storage usable, and can be serviced without cutting structural cabinet parts on site.

This playbook turns that promise into a controlled sequence: capture the project brief, verify material and hardware groups, work through three consequential choices, complete six observable phases, and close against a named finish line. It is designed to keep related resources together without pretending that a generic page knows the dimensions, loads, products, tools, site conditions, or requirements of a real job.

This is a planning playbook, not a dimensioned construction drawing, engineering review, code approval, machine instruction, or manufacturer authorization. Use it to organize questions and evidence. The released design, current product data, qualified review, field measurements, test pieces, and applicable requirements remain authoritative.

Private local working copy

Write the project brief before choosing a path

A useful brief records both the value and its source. Write the room, product, drawing, measurement method, responsible person, date, and revision where those details control the result. Entries remain in this browser when storage is available; they are not uploaded by this page.

Do not use a remembered dimension as a released input. If the source is missing, mark the field open and keep dependent purchasing, cutting, drilling, finishing, or installation on hold.

Material and hardware map

Verify purchasable groups, not just familiar names

The groups below are specification prompts, not a purchase order. Derive quantities from the released parts list, then reconcile actual stock size, measured thickness, face and grade requirements, grain direction, trim, kerf, defects, test pieces, hardware model, finish system, and supplier packaging before ordering.

A substitution is a design change when it alters thickness, stiffness, machining, fastener capacity, edge treatment, finish compatibility, reveal, movement, load, or installation sequence. Record the substitution, identify every dependent part or phase, and issue a new revision instead of quietly carrying an old quantity forward.

Choose, then reveal

Three decisions that change the project downstream

Select the condition that matches the verified brief before opening the consequence. This small pause prevents a familiar preference from masquerading as a project fact. The reveal explains what must be carried into dimensions, material, sequence, and evidence; it does not approve the choice.

Decision challenge 1 of 3

How will plumbing pass through the cabinet?

A large improvised cutout can destroy drawer space, back stiffness, and service access.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathMap a bounded service chase from real fixture locations: Openings stay as small as practical and avoid drawer boxes and attachment rails. Coordinate plumbing geometry before the interior is divided.

Conditional — verifyUse a removable false back around a known service zone: This preserves a finished interior when depth and access remain adequate. Confirm the false back does not crowd the trap or shutoffs.

Stop and resolveCut the cabinet after the plumber arrives: Unbounded field cuts can remove structure and finish without a documented plan. Do not use site improvisation as the primary service strategy.

Decision challenge 2 of 3

Where should drawer depth stop?

The sink bowl, drain, supplies, and countertop clips occupy a three-dimensional envelope.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathModel each drawer against the measured fixture envelope: Drawer length and notch strategy are set from the actual hardware and service space. Use sections, not only a front elevation, to protect moving storage.

Conditional — verifyUse one shallow top drawer and doors below: This reduces conflicts if the storage program accepts less drawer capacity. Verify the remaining drawer still clears sink clips and faucet hardware.

Stop and resolveOrder full-depth drawers before selecting the sink: The fixture can make finished drawers unusable. Freeze the fixture envelope before drawer production.

Decision challenge 3 of 3

How should exposed panel edges be protected?

Bathroom splashes and cleaning water concentrate at toe kicks, sink edges, and floor-adjacent seams.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathSeal every machined edge with a compatible documented system: Panel cores remain protected while future touchups are possible. Treat hidden cutouts and installation holes as exposed edges too.

Conditional — verifyUse factory-finished panels and matching edge banding: This is durable when seams, heat, pressure, and adhesive compatibility are controlled. Test one banded offcut through the expected moisture and cleaning cycle.

Stop and resolveRely on countertop caulk to protect the cabinet: Floor, plumbing, and cutout edges remain unprotected. One perimeter bead is not a cabinet moisture strategy.

After each reveal, record the verified condition in the brief and update every dependent dimension, material row, phase, and release check before continuing.

Six-phase path

Move from verified input to documented finish

Complete phases in the order shown unless the released project plan defines a safer dependency. Each checkbox represents a reviewed phase, not merely activity. Preserve the input, observed result, owner, date, revision, and exception record that lets another person reproduce why the work moved forward.

01

Phase 1 of 6 · 75–105 min

Measure

Outcome

Capture room, fixture, plumbing, and movement envelopes from the actual products and site.

Work sequence

  • Survey wall and floor variation.
  • Measure the sink, faucet, top, and clip zones.
  • Locate drains, supplies, door swing, and service access.
Evidence to keep

Coordinated plan and section showing every fixed and moving envelope.

Hold point

Stop if the sink, top, or plumbing locations are only nominal assumptions.

02

Phase 2 of 6 · 2–3 hr

Plan

Outcome

Divide the vanity into structure, storage, and service zones without hiding conflicts.

Work sequence

  • Choose doors, drawers, and back strategy.
  • Calculate hardware openings from actual thickness.
  • Define sealed edges and removable service panels.
Evidence to keep

Released cabinet sections, parts schedule, and service-opening dimensions.

Hold point

Stop if a drawer, rail, or fastener enters the fixture or plumbing envelope.

03

Phase 3 of 6 · 4–6 hr

Cut

Outcome

Produce moisture-protected, clearly handed cabinet and drawer parts.

Work sequence

  • Verify actual panel and edge-band thickness.
  • Cut box parts and bounded service openings.
  • Machine one drawer and front as the hardware first article.
Evidence to keep

Labeled parts, sealed test edge, and verified drawer-hardware sample.

Hold point

Stop if cut edges cannot be sealed before they become inaccessible.

04

Phase 4 of 6 · 3–5 hr

Assemble

Outcome

Build a square vanity and prove sink, drawer, and service access with reversible dry fits.

Work sequence

  • Assemble and square the box.
  • Fit drawers or doors with adjustment remaining.
  • Dry-fit the actual sink/top template and removable service panel.
Evidence to keep

Square cabinet with functioning storage and confirmed fixture clearances.

Hold point

Stop if the trap or shutoff path depends on removing a structural member.

05

Phase 5 of 6 · 2–4 hr plus trade coordination

Install

Outcome

Set the vanity level, anchor it, and coordinate professional service work without distorting the box.

Work sequence

  • Level at the floor high point.
  • Anchor through designed rails into verified structure.
  • Transfer bounded service openings and confirm countertop support.
Evidence to keep

Installed cabinet with level record, anchor locations, and service access photos.

Hold point

Stop before modifying plumbing, structure, or fixture instructions outside the approved scope.

06

Phase 6 of 6 · 45–75 min

Closeout

Outcome

Verify storage, water-adjacent details, service access, and the final fixture-to-cabinet relationship.

Work sequence

  • Cycle drawers and doors with plumbing complete.
  • Inspect sealed edges and splash paths.
  • Record product models, access panels, and maintenance touchups.
Evidence to keep

Function and moisture-boundary checklist with as-built service locations.

Hold point

Do not close while a leak, swelling edge, drawer conflict, or inaccessible shutoff remains.

Adapt without losing control

Useful variations and what they force you to revisit

A variation is not a cosmetic label. It can change the finished envelope, part set, sheet grouping, joinery, hardware, load, stability, transport, finish, installation, or removal path. Choose it early enough to update the authoritative project sources.

Floating vanity

Use when: Use only with a reviewed cabinet-to-wall load path and verified structural attachment behind the finished wall.

Carry through: The missing floor support increases rail, joint, fastener, plumbing, and service-access consequences throughout the box.

Door-and-shelf sink cabinet

Use when: Use when centered plumbing and stored items can share a serviceable open bay.

Carry through: Drawer conflicts disappear, but shelf shape, trap access, removable back, and leak inspection become the main interior decisions.

Drawer vanity with service notch

Use when: Use after the exact bowl, drain, valves, slide system, and connector movements are modeled in section.

Carry through: Each drawer needs a distinct usable shape and balance check rather than one repeated rectangular drawer box.

Catch rework while it is still a decision

Project risks, early signals, and hold actions

Use these risks as observation prompts rather than predictions. When a signal appears, protect the current condition, keep affected work visible, identify the smallest upstream cause, and repeat every dependent calculation, sample, fit, or inspection under the corrected revision.

Fixture selected after drawers

Early signal: The sink bowl, clips, faucet, trap, or supplies enter a completed drawer's travel.

Response: Hold installation, map the actual fixture envelope, and redesign affected drawer depth or arrangement before field cutting.

Oversized service opening

Early signal: A plumbing cut crosses a rail, corner, back attachment, or leaves an unsupported panel edge.

Response: Stop enlargement, restore a deliberate structural path, and use the smallest serviceable opening tied to measured plumbing.

Exposed wet panel core

Early signal: A cutout, toe, floor edge, or fastener hole shows absorbent core beside a splash or leak path.

Response: Dry the area, seal it with the approved edge system, and repeat inspection before the top or plumbing hides it.

Racked leveling

Early signal: Drawer travel or diagonals change as the vanity is pulled against wall or floor variation.

Response: Release attachment, level with shims at structural bearing points, restore square, and refasten without distortion.

Never solve a mismatch by trimming or forcing the downstream part without recording what changed. A local adjustment can hide a wrong datum, wrong product, wrong material thickness, unsafe clearance, blocked assembly sequence, or installation conflict that will reappear later.

Finish line

Close the project against observable results

The vanity fits the actual room and fixture stack while keeping storage smooth, wet edges protected, and every shutoff and trap serviceable.

  • The cabinet is level and square after anchoring, with the top supported at every designed bearing location.
  • Doors and drawers complete full travel without entering the sink, faucet, drain, supply, clip, or wall envelope.
  • Every machined edge beside plumbing or splash exposure has a continuous inspected protective finish.
  • Shutoffs, trap connections, and the removable service panel can be reached without removing a structural cabinet member.

Closeout should leave a usable trail for repair, repeat work, disassembly, warranty questions, and future changes. Save the released drawing or sketch, final parts and material records, product identities, approved exceptions, field changes, finish information, photographs where appropriate, and the final project-playbook CSV together.

Safety and authority boundaries

Use the playbook to expose uncertainty, not to override it

Follow current manufacturer instructions and use appropriate training, guarding, support, dust or fume control, ventilation, PPE, lifting methods, cure time, and disposal practices. Do not use this page as permission to operate unfamiliar equipment, defeat a safety system, exceed a tool or product rating, improvise a structural connection, or conceal work that has not passed the required inspection.

Structural, stair, guard, anchoring, seismic, electrical, plumbing, gas, fire, accessibility, and building-code decisions require the appropriate qualified professional or authority. Hardware clearances, fastener schedules, adhesive preparation, coating compatibility, load ratings, and installation methods come from the exact current product and project documentation. Verify them after any substitution.

Dimensions must name their datum, unit, finished or unfinished state, and measurement source. Check real openings at multiple locations; confirm square, level, plumb, flatness, obstructions, access, transport, assembly, service, and removal paths. Build a first article, sample, mockup, or dry fit when one failure could repeat across a batch or become expensive after finishing or installation.

Questions before starting

Project playbook FAQ

Can I build the bathroom vanity project playbook exactly as shown?

No. This page is a planning playbook rather than a dimensioned drawing or approval. Verify the real site, design, loads, material, hardware, joinery, tooling, finish, installation, and applicable requirements before irreversible work.

Where is my project progress saved?

When browser storage is available, progress stays locally in this browser under a versioned WoodCutTool record. This page does not require an account and does not send the brief, choices, or phase status to a server.

What should I do when a phase cannot be completed?

Keep the affected work on hold. Identify whether the mismatch comes from the design, measurement, material, hardware, setup, site, sequence, or current instructions. Correct the controlling source and repeat dependent checks before continuing.

Which CSV should I download?

The static project-playbook.csv is a blank durable record of the published brief, materials, decisions, and six phases. The progress CSV is created in your browser and includes the values and completion state currently entered on the page.
