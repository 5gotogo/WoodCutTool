The planning promise

Know the next decision before material, time, or site access is committed

Finish with a closet tower that can enter the room, stand up under the ceiling, anchor securely, and give every shelf, rod, and drawer its promised clearance.

This playbook turns that promise into a controlled sequence: capture the project brief, verify material and hardware groups, work through three consequential choices, complete six observable phases, and close against a named finish line. It is designed to keep related resources together without pretending that a generic page knows the dimensions, loads, products, tools, site conditions, or requirements of a real job.

This is a planning playbook, not a dimensioned construction drawing, engineering review, code approval, machine instruction, or manufacturer authorization. Use it to organize questions and evidence. The released design, current product data, qualified review, field measurements, test pieces, and applicable requirements remain authoritative.

Private local working copy

Write the project brief before choosing a path

A useful brief records both the value and its source. Write the room, product, drawing, measurement method, responsible person, date, and revision where those details control the result. Entries remain in this browser when storage is available; they are not uploaded by this page.

Do not use a remembered dimension as a released input. If the source is missing, mark the field open and keep dependent purchasing, cutting, drilling, finishing, or installation on hold.

Material and hardware map

Verify purchasable groups, not just familiar names

The groups below are specification prompts, not a purchase order. Derive quantities from the released parts list, then reconcile actual stock size, measured thickness, face and grade requirements, grain direction, trim, kerf, defects, test pieces, hardware model, finish system, and supplier packaging before ordering.

Material or hardware groupSpecification to verify
Tower panels3/4 in plywood or finished panels for sides, fixed shelves, and base
Adjustable shelvesThickness and edge build-up checked for the intended folded load and span
Drawer componentsDrawer stock, bottoms, confirmed slides, fronts, and pulls
Closet hardwareRods, sockets, shelf pins, brackets, and anti-tip attachment components
Fit partsFiller, scribe, base, and top trim sized from the field survey

A substitution is a design change when it alters thickness, stiffness, machining, fastener capacity, edge treatment, finish compatibility, reveal, movement, load, or installation sequence. Record the substitution, identify every dependent part or phase, and issue a new revision instead of quietly carrying an old quantity forward.

Choose, then reveal

Three decisions that change the project downstream

Select the condition that matches the verified brief before opening the consequence. This small pause prevents a familiar preference from masquerading as a project fact. The reveal explains what must be carried into dimensions, material, sequence, and evidence; it does not approve the choice.

Decision challenge 1 of 3

Should the tower be built in one piece?

The finished diagonal and turning path can exceed the room even when the tower fits upright.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathSplit at a designed fixed-shelf or connector plane: Each module can travel safely and the assembled load path remains explicit. Let the delivery path determine module breaks before cutting.

Conditional — verifyAssemble the tall case inside the closet: This can work when staging, fastening access, and wall protection are planned. Sequence every inaccessible fastener before committing.

Stop and resolveBuild full height and hope it tips up: The ceiling or doorway can trap a completed tower. Prove the diagonal and turn, not just the standing height.

Decision challenge 2 of 3

How will hanging and shelf zones be allocated?

Clothing dimensions, rod height, shelf thickness, and drawer fronts compete for the same vertical space.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathLay out from measured wardrobe categories: Each zone has a real item count and usable clearance. Design storage around contents rather than evenly divided openings.

Conditional — verifyUse adjustable zones within a fixed structural frame: This preserves flexibility if fixed shelves and rod supports remain adequate. Keep adjustment holes clear of connectors and drawer hardware.

Stop and resolveDivide the tower into equal boxes: Equal openings may waste long-hang height and crowd folded stacks. Visual symmetry is not a storage program.

Decision challenge 3 of 3

Where will room variation be absorbed?

Closet walls and floors are rarely square enough for a full-width rigid assembly.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathUse level base plus side and top fit parts: The storage modules stay square while trim meets the room. Preserve installation tolerance outside moving hardware.

Conditional — verifyLeave a deliberate open reveal at one side: This can suit a freestanding look when the gap, restraint, and cleaning access are intentional. Document the reveal as a design choice, not a measuring accident.

Stop and resolveWedge the tower between both walls: The case can rack and doors or drawers lose alignment. Do not use compression against unknown walls as installation control.

After each reveal, record the verified condition in the brief and update every dependent dimension, material row, phase, and release check before continuing.

Six-phase path

Move from verified input to documented finish

Complete phases in the order shown unless the released project plan defines a safer dependency. Each checkbox represents a reviewed phase, not merely activity. Preserve the input, observed result, owner, date, revision, and exception record that lets another person reproduce why the work moved forward.

01

Phase 1 of 6 · 75–105 min

Measure

Outcome

Capture the closet envelope, wardrobe inventory, delivery path, and verified anchor zones.

Work sequence

  • Survey walls, floor, ceiling, trim, and doors.
  • Measure representative clothing and storage bins.
  • Calculate tip-up and module transport envelopes.
Evidence to keep

Closet elevation plus wardrobe and delivery-path records.

Hold point

Stop if ceiling low point, doorway clearance, or wall structure is unverified.

02

Phase 2 of 6 · 2–4 hr

Plan

Outcome

Convert storage demand into modular zones, hardware clearances, fit parts, and installation sequence.

Work sequence

  • Set long-hang, double-hang, shelves, and drawers.
  • Place fixed shelves and module joints.
  • Allocate base, fillers, restraint, and installation access.
Evidence to keep

Released elevation, module sections, and hardware schedule.

Hold point

Stop if a drawer, rod, or module cannot be assembled and serviced in sequence.

03

Phase 3 of 6 · 4–6 hr

Cut

Outcome

Produce paired sides, aligned shelf-pin rows, square modules, and clearly separated fit parts.

Work sequence

  • Cut sides in matched pairs.
  • Bore pin rows from one bottom/front datum.
  • Make a first drawer or connector sample.
Evidence to keep

Labeled modules with matching pin patterns and passing hardware sample.

Hold point

Stop if handed panels or module break references become ambiguous.

04

Phase 4 of 6 · 4–6 hr

Assemble

Outcome

Build square transportable modules and prove drawers, shelves, and rods before entering the closet.

Work sequence

  • Assemble each case and lock square with its back.
  • Fit drawers and one adjustable shelf.
  • Dry-connect stacked modules and verify the total height.
Evidence to keep

Square modules with recorded diagonals and complete dry-stack height.

Hold point

Stop if stacked joints depend on wall pressure or hidden inaccessible fasteners.

05

Phase 5 of 6 · 3–5 hr

Install

Outcome

Level, stack, connect, anchor, and fit the system without losing door or drawer clearance.

Work sequence

  • Set and level the base at the high point.
  • Move modules through the verified path and connect them.
  • Anchor to structure, then scribe fillers and install rods.
Evidence to keep

Anchored system with level, restraint, filler, and movement-clearance evidence.

Hold point

Stop if a module cannot be restrained before the next one is lifted into place.

06

Phase 6 of 6 · 45–75 min

Closeout

Outcome

Load representative contents, test moving components, and record adjustment and capacity limits.

Work sequence

  • Cycle drawers and doors under normal load.
  • Check rod deflection and shelf support.
  • Label adjustable parts and retain hardware information.
Evidence to keep

Loaded function test and final closet configuration record.

Hold point

Do not close while a tower moves, a drawer rubs, or a rod support loosens.

Adapt without losing control

Useful variations and what they force you to revisit

A variation is not a cosmetic label. It can change the finished envelope, part set, sheet grouping, joinery, hardware, load, stability, transport, finish, installation, or removal path. Choose it early enough to update the authoritative project sources.

Low-ceiling split tower

Use when: Use when the standing tower fits but its tip-up diagonal or doorway turn does not.

Carry through: A designed horizontal joint, connector access, and stacking restraint divide the case into transportable modules.

Drawer tower between double-hang bays

Use when: Use when measured garments leave enough vertical space for drawers and two independent rod zones.

Carry through: Drawer fronts, rod sockets, shelf thickness, and clothing drop must share one reconciled elevation.

Removable-fit renter tower

Use when: Use where wall work is limited but an allowed anti-tip method and deliberate side reveals are available.

Carry through: Removable fillers handle room variation while the tower still needs positive restraint and clean disassembly.

Catch rework while it is still a decision

Project risks, early signals, and hold actions

Use these risks as observation prompts rather than predictions. When a signal appears, protect the current condition, keep affected work visible, identify the smallest upstream cause, and repeat every dependent calculation, sample, fit, or inspection under the corrected revision.

Trapped full-height case

Early signal: The delivery mockup or diagonal calculation touches ceiling, door head, corner, or closet opening.

Response: Hold fabrication, introduce a designed module break, and rerun the route with the largest finished module.

Mirrored pin rows

Early signal: Shelf pins form mismatched elevations or front/rear offsets across paired tower sides.

Response: Quarantine the pair, confirm face and bottom datums, and recut or repair only through a verified symmetric jig plan.

Installed case rack

Early signal: Drawer travel, shelf seating, or diagonals change when the tower meets baseboard or floor variation.

Response: Release restraint, level the base and fit external trim, restore case square, then re-anchor.

Rod-drawer collision

Early signal: Hanging garments, rod socket screws, or rod support enters drawer travel or usable opening.

Response: Stop hardware installation and reconcile the complete vertical and depth sections before drilling more parts.

Never solve a mismatch by trimming or forcing the downstream part without recording what changed. A local adjustment can hide a wrong datum, wrong product, wrong material thickness, unsafe clearance, blocked assembly sequence, or installation conflict that will reappear later.

Finish line

Close the project against observable results

The closet tower reaches the room in proven modules, stands square on its own leveled base, and serves the measured wardrobe without hardware collisions.

  • Every finished module travels through the recorded route and connects without forcing walls, ceiling, or case joints.
  • The installed tower remains level and plumb with positive restraint at the documented structure.
  • Drawers complete full loaded travel, shelves sit at matching pin elevations, and rods remain secure under representative clothing.
  • Long-hang, double-hang, folded, shoe, and drawer zones retain the clear dimensions promised in the wardrobe brief.

Closeout should leave a usable trail for repair, repeat work, disassembly, warranty questions, and future changes. Save the released drawing or sketch, final parts and material records, product identities, approved exceptions, field changes, finish information, photographs where appropriate, and the final project-playbook CSV together.

Safety and authority boundaries

Use the playbook to expose uncertainty, not to override it

Follow current manufacturer instructions and use appropriate training, guarding, support, dust or fume control, ventilation, PPE, lifting methods, cure time, and disposal practices. Do not use this page as permission to operate unfamiliar equipment, defeat a safety system, exceed a tool or product rating, improvise a structural connection, or conceal work that has not passed the required inspection.

Structural, stair, guard, anchoring, seismic, electrical, plumbing, gas, fire, accessibility, and building-code decisions require the appropriate qualified professional or authority. Hardware clearances, fastener schedules, adhesive preparation, coating compatibility, load ratings, and installation methods come from the exact current product and project documentation. Verify them after any substitution.

Dimensions must name their datum, unit, finished or unfinished state, and measurement source. Check real openings at multiple locations; confirm square, level, plumb, flatness, obstructions, access, transport, assembly, service, and removal paths. Build a first article, sample, mockup, or dry fit when one failure could repeat across a batch or become expensive after finishing or installation.

Questions before starting

Project playbook FAQ

Can I build the closet tower project playbook exactly as shown?

No. This page is a planning playbook rather than a dimensioned drawing or approval. Verify the real site, design, loads, material, hardware, joinery, tooling, finish, installation, and applicable requirements before irreversible work.

Where is my project progress saved?

When browser storage is available, progress stays locally in this browser under a versioned WoodCutTool record. This page does not require an account and does not send the brief, choices, or phase status to a server.

What should I do when a phase cannot be completed?

Keep the affected work on hold. Identify whether the mismatch comes from the design, measurement, material, hardware, setup, site, sequence, or current instructions. Correct the controlling source and repeat dependent checks before continuing.

Which CSV should I download?

The static project-playbook.csv is a blank durable record of the published brief, materials, decisions, and six phases. The progress CSV is created in your browser and includes the values and completion state currently entered on the page.
