The planning promise

Know the next decision before material, time, or site access is committed

Finish with a stable table whose proportions were tested in the room and whose top, base, and finish can handle everyday use without a hidden weak joint.

This playbook turns that promise into a controlled sequence: capture the project brief, verify material and hardware groups, work through three consequential choices, complete six observable phases, and close against a named finish line. It is designed to keep related resources together without pretending that a generic page knows the dimensions, loads, products, tools, site conditions, or requirements of a real job.

This is a planning playbook, not a dimensioned construction drawing, engineering review, code approval, machine instruction, or manufacturer authorization. Use it to organize questions and evidence. The released design, current product data, qualified review, field measurements, test pieces, and applicable requirements remain authoritative.

Private local working copy

Write the project brief before choosing a path

A useful brief records both the value and its source. Write the room, product, drawing, measurement method, responsible person, date, and revision where those details control the result. Entries remain in this browser when storage is available; they are not uploaded by this page.

Do not use a remembered dimension as a released input. If the source is missing, mark the field open and keep dependent purchasing, cutting, drilling, finishing, or installation on hold.

Material and hardware map

Verify purchasable groups, not just familiar names

The groups below are specification prompts, not a purchase order. Derive quantities from the released parts list, then reconcile actual stock size, measured thickness, face and grade requirements, grain direction, trim, kerf, defects, test pieces, hardware model, finish system, and supplier packaging before ordering.

Material or hardware groupSpecification to verify
Top panelsFlat plywood skins or selected boards with a documented flattening and edge strategy
Base membersStraight plywood laminations or furniture-grade lumber sized for racking loads
JoineryConfirmed connectors, glue, dowels, loose tenons, or screws with adequate edge distance
Finish systemSample-approved sealer and topcoat suitable for heat, moisture, and cleaning

A substitution is a design change when it alters thickness, stiffness, machining, fastener capacity, edge treatment, finish compatibility, reveal, movement, load, or installation sequence. Record the substitution, identify every dependent part or phase, and issue a new revision instead of quietly carrying an old quantity forward.

Choose, then reveal

Three decisions that change the project downstream

Select the condition that matches the verified brief before opening the consequence. This small pause prevents a familiar preference from masquerading as a project fact. The reveal explains what must be carried into dimensions, material, sequence, and evidence; it does not approve the choice.

Decision challenge 1 of 3

How will the top stay flat?

A wide visual slab can telegraph panel bow, unbalanced finish, or a twisted base.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathUse flat stock, balanced construction, and slotted or compatible attachment: Top and base can remain aligned without forcing one to correct the other. Prove flatness before and after the full finish schedule.

Conditional — verifyLaminate two plywood layers on a verified flat caul: This creates thickness and stiffness when pressure is even and edges remain aligned. Dry-clamp the full stack and plan squeeze-out before glue.

Stop and resolvePull a bowed top flat with base screws: The fasteners store stress and may distort or fail later. Correct the panel or construction instead of using hardware as a clamp forever.

Decision challenge 2 of 3

What makes the base resist racking?

Four legs connected only to a top can loosen when the table is pushed sideways.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathUse aprons, stretchers, or a rigid panel base with tested joints: Lateral loads travel through deliberate triangulation or moment-resistant connections. Identify the member that resists force in each direction.

Conditional — verifyUse wide leg plates with rated hardware: This can suit a light table if the mounting area and fasteners are proven. Test repeated side loading before finishing.

Stop and resolveRely on glue at four small end-grain contacts: The joint offers little long-term resistance to leverage. Do not confuse initial stiffness with durable racking strength.

Decision challenge 3 of 3

When should the final edge profile be chosen?

Rounded corners, veneer thickness, banding, and the finish sample all change how the table feels and wears.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathTest the complete edge and finish on a full-thickness offcut: Touch, color, impact behavior, and veneer exposure are visible before the top is committed. Make the sample with the same tool sequence as the table.

Conditional — verifyUse a replaceable solid-wood edge: This can improve durability when movement and miter or butt joints are detailed. Match grain, moisture, and attachment to the panel construction.

Stop and resolveRound through thin veneer after assembly: The substrate may appear at the most visible contact edge. Know the face and edge build-up before routing.

After each reveal, record the verified condition in the brief and update every dependent dimension, material row, phase, and release check before continuing.

Six-phase path

Move from verified input to documented finish

Complete phases in the order shown unless the released project plan defines a safer dependency. Each checkbox represents a reviewed phase, not merely activity. Preserve the input, observed result, owner, date, revision, and exception record that lets another person reproduce why the work moved forward.

01

Phase 1 of 6 · 30–60 min

Measure

Outcome

Fit the table to the sofa, room traffic, users, and daily objects.

Work sequence

  • Measure seating and reach heights.
  • Map walking and furniture movement.
  • Record point loads and surface hazards.
Evidence to keep

Scaled room sketch and declared table-use boundary.

Hold point

Stop if the chosen footprint blocks a required walkway or moving seat.

02

Phase 2 of 6 · 90–150 min

Plan

Outcome

Resolve proportion, flatness, racking resistance, joinery, edge treatment, and finish.

Work sequence

  • Mock up top size and height.
  • Draw the lateral load path through the base.
  • Make an edge-and-finish sample plan.
Evidence to keep

Dimensioned table with joint, attachment, and finish notes.

Hold point

Stop if the top-to-base attachment must hide panel bow or joint error.

03

Phase 3 of 6 · 2–4 hr

Cut

Outcome

Create flat, matched top and base components with clean visible edges.

Work sequence

  • Cut top layers oversized for lamination if needed.
  • Mill paired base members from shared references.
  • Produce the full-thickness edge sample.
Evidence to keep

Flat component set and approved edge/finish sample.

Hold point

Stop on twist, mismatched paired legs, or veneer breakthrough.

04

Phase 4 of 6 · 2–4 hr plus cure

Assemble

Outcome

Build a square, rigid base and attach a flat top without introducing stress.

Work sequence

  • Dry-fit and diagonal-check the base.
  • Glue or fasten joints in a controlled sequence.
  • Center and attach the top with the planned allowance.
Evidence to keep

Table standing flat with passing side-load and diagonal checks.

Hold point

Stop if one foot lifts or any joint opens under moderate hand pressure.

05

Phase 5 of 6 · 20–40 min

Install

Outcome

Place and level the freestanding table in its actual use zone without floor damage.

Work sequence

  • Install feet or glides.
  • Verify sofa reach and walkway clearance.
  • Check edge and corner contact from normal seated paths.
Evidence to keep

Final room-clearance and floor-contact record.

Hold point

Stop if a sharp projection enters the normal walking or reclining envelope.

06

Phase 6 of 6 · 30–45 min

Closeout

Outcome

Verify stability and finish resistance, then retain the finish recipe for repairs.

Work sequence

  • Repeat side-load and point-load checks.
  • Test water cleanup on the sample.
  • Record finish products, coat dates, and care limits.
Evidence to keep

Function check and finish-maintenance note.

Hold point

Do not close while the table rocks, finish remains uncured, or joints continue moving.

Adapt without losing control

Useful variations and what they force you to revisit

A variation is not a cosmetic label. It can change the finished envelope, part set, sheet grouping, joinery, hardware, load, stability, transport, finish, installation, or removal path. Choose it early enough to update the authoritative project sources.

Plywood waterfall profile

Use when: Use when matched outside faces and a continuous top-to-side visual line matter more than removable legs.

Carry through: Miter or fold geometry, veneer sequence, clamp access, and edge impact become the defining build controls.

Open-shelf base

Use when: Use when books or trays need a lower plane and the center support fits knee and vacuum clearances.

Carry through: The shelf and divider can resist racking, but they change leg space, visual mass, and finish access.

Knock-down base

Use when: Use when the table must move through a constrained route or be flat-packed without weakening repeated assembly points.

Carry through: Rated inserts, bolt access, anti-rotation details, and an owner assembly sequence replace permanent glued joints.

Catch rework while it is still a decision

Project risks, early signals, and hold actions

Use these risks as observation prompts rather than predictions. When a signal appears, protect the current condition, keep affected work visible, identify the smallest upstream cause, and repeat every dependent calculation, sample, fit, or inspection under the corrected revision.

Untested room proportion

Early signal: The top blocks the walking path, sits beyond comfortable reach, or conflicts with reclining furniture.

Response: Return to a full-size floor and height mockup and revise the top before cutting or laminating finish material.

Decorative but weak base

Early signal: The table sways laterally or a joint line opens when the top edge is pushed.

Response: Unload the table and add a deliberate apron, stretcher, panel, or redesigned joint along the observed force direction.

Veneer breakthrough

Early signal: Routing or sanding exposes substrate at the rounded top edge or corner.

Response: Stop profiling, use the approved full-thickness edge sample, and change the edge build-up rather than blending through veneer.

Top pulled out of flat

Early signal: A foot lifts or the top twists after attachment or after one finish face cures.

Response: Release stressed attachment, restore balanced finishing and a flat top, then reattach without forcing top or base.

Never solve a mismatch by trimming or forcing the downstream part without recording what changed. A local adjustment can hide a wrong datum, wrong product, wrong material thickness, unsafe clearance, blocked assembly sequence, or installation conflict that will reappear later.

Finish line

Close the project against observable results

The coffee table fits its seating group, stands without stored stress, and handles everyday edge, surface, and lateral loads through observable structure.

  • All feet remain in contact on the intended floor and the table does not rock after the top is attached.
  • A moderate push from each long and short direction produces no opening joint or persistent rack.
  • The approved edge profile shows no exposed substrate and every normal contact corner feels consistent.
  • The cured finish passes the planned water-cleanup check on the matched sample and completed top.

Closeout should leave a usable trail for repair, repeat work, disassembly, warranty questions, and future changes. Save the released drawing or sketch, final parts and material records, product identities, approved exceptions, field changes, finish information, photographs where appropriate, and the final project-playbook CSV together.

Safety and authority boundaries

Use the playbook to expose uncertainty, not to override it

Follow current manufacturer instructions and use appropriate training, guarding, support, dust or fume control, ventilation, PPE, lifting methods, cure time, and disposal practices. Do not use this page as permission to operate unfamiliar equipment, defeat a safety system, exceed a tool or product rating, improvise a structural connection, or conceal work that has not passed the required inspection.

Structural, stair, guard, anchoring, seismic, electrical, plumbing, gas, fire, accessibility, and building-code decisions require the appropriate qualified professional or authority. Hardware clearances, fastener schedules, adhesive preparation, coating compatibility, load ratings, and installation methods come from the exact current product and project documentation. Verify them after any substitution.

Dimensions must name their datum, unit, finished or unfinished state, and measurement source. Check real openings at multiple locations; confirm square, level, plumb, flatness, obstructions, access, transport, assembly, service, and removal paths. Build a first article, sample, mockup, or dry fit when one failure could repeat across a batch or become expensive after finishing or installation.

Questions before starting

Project playbook FAQ

Can I build the coffee table project playbook exactly as shown?

No. This page is a planning playbook rather than a dimensioned drawing or approval. Verify the real site, design, loads, material, hardware, joinery, tooling, finish, installation, and applicable requirements before irreversible work.

Where is my project progress saved?

When browser storage is available, progress stays locally in this browser under a versioned WoodCutTool record. This page does not require an account and does not send the brief, choices, or phase status to a server.

What should I do when a phase cannot be completed?

Keep the affected work on hold. Identify whether the mismatch comes from the design, measurement, material, hardware, setup, site, sequence, or current instructions. Correct the controlling source and repeat dependent checks before continuing.

Which CSV should I download?

The static project-playbook.csv is a blank durable record of the published brief, materials, decisions, and six phases. The progress CSV is created in your browser and includes the values and completion state currently entered on the page.
