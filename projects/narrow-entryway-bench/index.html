The planning promise

Know the next decision before material, time, or site access is committed

Finish with a shallow bench that welcomes a seated user, holds the measured shoes, clears the door and trim, and remains stable on the real entry floor.

This playbook turns that promise into a controlled sequence: capture the project brief, verify material and hardware groups, work through three consequential choices, complete six observable phases, and close against a named finish line. It is designed to keep related resources together without pretending that a generic page knows the dimensions, loads, products, tools, site conditions, or requirements of a real job.

This is a planning playbook, not a dimensioned construction drawing, engineering review, code approval, machine instruction, or manufacturer authorization. Use it to organize questions and evidence. The released design, current product data, qualified review, field measurements, test pieces, and applicable requirements remain authoritative.

Private local working copy

Write the project brief before choosing a path

A useful brief records both the value and its source. Write the room, product, drawing, measurement method, responsible person, date, and revision where those details control the result. Entries remain in this browser when storage is available; they are not uploaded by this page.

Do not use a remembered dimension as a released input. If the source is missing, mark the field open and keep dependent purchasing, cutting, drilling, finishing, or installation on hold.

Choose, then reveal

Three decisions that change the project downstream

Select the condition that matches the verified brief before opening the consequence. This small pause prevents a familiar preference from masquerading as a project fact. The reveal explains what must be carried into dimensions, material, sequence, and evidence; it does not approve the choice.

Decision challenge 1 of 3

What controls bench depth?

A deeper seat may feel comfortable but can narrow the everyday path and enter the door swing.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathMock up the full bench with the door and a seated user: Comfort and circulation are tested at the same time. Use the smallest depth that serves the actual sitting task.

Conditional — verifyUse a slightly sloped or rounded shallow seat: This can improve comfort when structure and cushion remain secure. Test the front edge and heel clearance before cutting.

Stop and resolveMatch a standard dining bench depth: The entry may lose required circulation and door clearance. Room movement outranks catalog furniture proportions.

Decision challenge 2 of 3

How should wet shoes be stored?

Water, salt, and dirt concentrate on lower edges and enclosed shelves.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathUse removable trays, ventilation, and sealed cleanable surfaces: Moisture is contained and the interior can be inspected. Make the dirty component removable without unloading the entire bench.

Conditional — verifyUse open slatted shelves above a protected floor mat: This works when drips cannot damage wall, floor, or structural edges. Check the cleaning path and toe clearance.

Stop and resolveEnclose wet footwear in an unvented plywood box: Moisture and odor remain against vulnerable panel edges. Do not design sealed storage for wet contents.

Decision challenge 3 of 3

Does the bench need restraint?

A shallow footprint, lid, tall coat panel, or child climbing can move the center of gravity outward.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathTest edge loading and anchor when the use case can tip or slide: Restraint responds to measured geometry and behavior. Include the open lid or coat panel in the worst case.

Conditional — verifyUse a low open bench with wide feet after a documented stability test: This can remain freestanding if it passes on the actual floor. Recheck after adding glides or a cushion.

Stop and resolveAssume the wall will catch it: Unplanned impact can injure users and damage trim. The wall is not a restraint system.

After each reveal, record the verified condition in the brief and update every dependent dimension, material row, phase, and release check before continuing.

Six-phase path

Move from verified input to documented finish

Complete phases in the order shown unless the released project plan defines a safer dependency. Each checkbox represents a reviewed phase, not merely activity. Preserve the input, observed result, owner, date, revision, and exception record that lets another person reproduce why the work moved forward.

01

Phase 1 of 6 · 45–75 min

Measure

Outcome

Capture door, path, seated user, shoes, wet zone, floor, and wall constraints.

Work sequence

  • Trace the complete door swing.
  • Mock up seat and walking clearances.
  • Inventory footwear and room obstructions.
Evidence to keep

Entry plan showing simultaneous movement and storage envelopes.

Hold point

Stop if the proposed bench narrows a required path or covers a service.

02

Phase 2 of 6 · 90–150 min

Plan

Outcome

Resolve shallow structure, seating load, shoe access, moisture handling, base, and restraint.

Work sequence

  • Place dividers under the seat load.
  • Design removable trays or open shelves.
  • Allocate baseboard gap, leveling, and anti-tip points.
Evidence to keep

Bench section with circulation, load, and cleaning paths.

Hold point

Stop if the longest shoe cannot be removed while the door and bench stay in normal position.

03

Phase 3 of 6 · 2–4 hr

Cut

Outcome

Produce compact structural parts and seal the edges most exposed to entry moisture.

Work sequence

  • Cut paired ends and seat supports.
  • Prepare removable tray or shelf components.
  • Make a lower-edge finish sample.
Evidence to keep

Labeled parts and approved moisture-edge sample.

Hold point

Stop on insufficient seat bearing, sharp aisle edges, or unprotected panel core.

04

Phase 4 of 6 · 2–4 hr

Assemble

Outcome

Build a square shallow bench and prove seat stiffness, shoe retrieval, and stability.

Work sequence

  • Assemble base, dividers, rails, and seat.
  • Fit trays and ventilation gaps.
  • Perform unloaded and moderate edge-load tests.
Evidence to keep

Stable bench with functioning storage and cleanable interior.

Hold point

Stop if the bench rocks, racks, or shifts during entry and exit.

05

Phase 5 of 6 · 45–90 min

Install

Outcome

Place the bench around floor and baseboard variation without sacrificing the surveyed path.

Work sequence

  • Level feet or fit the plinth.
  • Verify door and seated clearances.
  • Install restraint into verified structure if required.
Evidence to keep

Final clearance, level, and restraint record.

Hold point

Stop if installation moves the bench into the door arc or blocks ventilation.

06

Phase 6 of 6 · 30–60 min

Closeout

Outcome

Test loaded seating, shoe use, cleaning, and everyday entry movement.

Work sequence

  • Load the seat progressively.
  • Store and retrieve every shoe class.
  • Run the door and walking-path test with normal bags and coats.
Evidence to keep

Use test plus cleaning and finish-care note.

Hold point

Do not close while moisture pools, the bench shifts, or the door contacts any part.

Adapt without losing control

Useful variations and what they force you to revisit

A variation is not a cosmetic label. It can change the finished envelope, part set, sheet grouping, joinery, hardware, load, stability, transport, finish, installation, or removal path. Choose it early enough to update the authoritative project sources.

Open tray shoe bench

Use when: Use when wet footwear needs immediate ventilation and removable trays can exit toward the room without blocking the path.

Carry through: Cleaning improves, while shelf lips, drip containment, lower-edge finish, and visible shoe order become prominent.

Dry-gear lift-lid bench

Use when: Use only for contents that enter dry and with a lid that clears door, wall, and seated users.

Carry through: Hidden volume increases, but lid control, open-state stability, and finger clearance become release gates.

Bench with separate coat rail

Use when: Use when verified wall structure can carry hanging and climbing misuse independently of the shallow bench.

Carry through: Vertical storage expands without making the bench top-heavy, but two attachment and clearance systems must align.

Catch rework while it is still a decision

Project risks, early signals, and hold actions

Use these risks as observation prompts rather than predictions. When a signal appears, protect the current condition, keep affected work visible, identify the smallest upstream cause, and repeat every dependent calculation, sample, fit, or inspection under the corrected revision.

Circulation lost to seat comfort

Early signal: A seated knee, open door, bag, or passing user reduces the surveyed path below its required clearance.

Response: Return to the room mockup and reduce or relocate the bench rather than accepting a permanent bottleneck.

Wet-edge exposure

Early signal: Water beads or staining appears at shelf edges, feet, joints, or the panel core after shoe storage.

Response: Remove wet contents, dry and inspect the part, then restore the approved finish or replace the vulnerable component.

Trim omitted from footprint

Early signal: The case stands proud, door contacts it, or the bench cannot sit level because baseboard or casing occupies the planned space.

Response: Stop installation and revise the plinth, back clearance, or overall depth from the actual trim survey.

Shallow edge-load instability

Early signal: The bench slides, rocks, or lifts a rear foot as a user sits near the front edge.

Response: Remove use load and revise feet, base, footprint, or positive restraint before repeating the staged seat test.

Never solve a mismatch by trimming or forcing the downstream part without recording what changed. A local adjustment can hide a wrong datum, wrong product, wrong material thickness, unsafe clearance, blocked assembly sequence, or installation conflict that will reappear later.

Finish line

Close the project against observable results

The narrow bench earns its entry footprint by preserving movement while providing stable seating and storage that can be cleaned after real footwear use.

  • The door reaches its required open position and the surveyed walking path remains clear with a representative user seated.
  • The front-edge and distributed seat-load tests cause no slide, rock, foot lift, rack, or persistent seat deflection.
  • Every measured shoe and removable tray enters and exits without contacting the door, divider, or seated user's legs.
  • Water placed in the wet-storage test remains contained and removable without reaching unsealed panel core or the room floor.

Closeout should leave a usable trail for repair, repeat work, disassembly, warranty questions, and future changes. Save the released drawing or sketch, final parts and material records, product identities, approved exceptions, field changes, finish information, photographs where appropriate, and the final project-playbook CSV together.

Safety and authority boundaries

Use the playbook to expose uncertainty, not to override it

Follow current manufacturer instructions and use appropriate training, guarding, support, dust or fume control, ventilation, PPE, lifting methods, cure time, and disposal practices. Do not use this page as permission to operate unfamiliar equipment, defeat a safety system, exceed a tool or product rating, improvise a structural connection, or conceal work that has not passed the required inspection.

Structural, stair, guard, anchoring, seismic, electrical, plumbing, gas, fire, accessibility, and building-code decisions require the appropriate qualified professional or authority. Hardware clearances, fastener schedules, adhesive preparation, coating compatibility, load ratings, and installation methods come from the exact current product and project documentation. Verify them after any substitution.

Dimensions must name their datum, unit, finished or unfinished state, and measurement source. Check real openings at multiple locations; confirm square, level, plumb, flatness, obstructions, access, transport, assembly, service, and removal paths. Build a first article, sample, mockup, or dry fit when one failure could repeat across a batch or become expensive after finishing or installation.

Questions before starting

Project playbook FAQ

Can I build the narrow entryway bench project playbook exactly as shown?

No. This page is a planning playbook rather than a dimensioned drawing or approval. Verify the real site, design, loads, material, hardware, joinery, tooling, finish, installation, and applicable requirements before irreversible work.

Where is my project progress saved?

When browser storage is available, progress stays locally in this browser under a versioned WoodCutTool record. This page does not require an account and does not send the brief, choices, or phase status to a server.

What should I do when a phase cannot be completed?

Keep the affected work on hold. Identify whether the mismatch comes from the design, measurement, material, hardware, setup, site, sequence, or current instructions. Correct the controlling source and repeat dependent checks before continuing.

Which CSV should I download?

The static project-playbook.csv is a blank durable record of the published brief, materials, decisions, and six phases. The progress CSV is created in your browser and includes the values and completion state currently entered on the page.
