The planning promise

Know the next decision before material, time, or site access is committed

Finish with a nightstand that lands at the intended reach height, opens fully beside the bed, and routes power without sacrificing drawer travel or back stiffness.

This playbook turns that promise into a controlled sequence: capture the project brief, verify material and hardware groups, work through three consequential choices, complete six observable phases, and close against a named finish line. It is designed to keep related resources together without pretending that a generic page knows the dimensions, loads, products, tools, site conditions, or requirements of a real job.

This is a planning playbook, not a dimensioned construction drawing, engineering review, code approval, machine instruction, or manufacturer authorization. Use it to organize questions and evidence. The released design, current product data, qualified review, field measurements, test pieces, and applicable requirements remain authoritative.

Private local working copy

Write the project brief before choosing a path

A useful brief records both the value and its source. Write the room, product, drawing, measurement method, responsible person, date, and revision where those details control the result. Entries remain in this browser when storage is available; they are not uploaded by this page.

Do not use a remembered dimension as a released input. If the source is missing, mark the field open and keep dependent purchasing, cutting, drilling, finishing, or installation on hold.

Material and hardware map

The groups below are specification prompts, not a purchase order. Derive quantities from the released parts list, then reconcile actual stock size, measured thickness, face and grade requirements, grain direction, trim, kerf, defects, test pieces, hardware model, finish system, and supplier packaging before ordering.

A substitution is a design change when it alters thickness, stiffness, machining, fastener capacity, edge treatment, finish compatibility, reveal, movement, load, or installation sequence. Record the substitution, identify every dependent part or phase, and issue a new revision instead of quietly carrying an old quantity forward.

Choose, then reveal

Three decisions that change the project downstream

Select the condition that matches the verified brief before opening the consequence. This small pause prevents a familiar preference from masquerading as a project fact. The reveal explains what must be carried into dimensions, material, sequence, and evidence; it does not approve the choice.

Decision challenge 1 of 3

What sets the nightstand height?

Catalog dimensions do not know the actual mattress, topper, frame, or preferred reach.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathMock up the top beside the made bed: Reach, lamp position, and bedding clearance are tested in the real posture. Use the room and user, not a generic furniture height.

Conditional — verifyMatch the top within a small range of mattress height: This is a useful starting point when the user tests the final mockup. Keep feet or plinth adjustment available until the bed is final.

Stop and resolveCopy a nightstand height before the mattress arrives: The result may be awkward or blocked by the bed frame. Freeze height only after the bed system is measured.

Decision challenge 2 of 3

How should charging cables cross the case?

A casual hole can weaken the back, trap a plug, abrade insulation, or block a drawer.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathUse a protected path outside drawer and structural zones: Cable bend, plug size, strain relief, and service removal are all visible. Route the largest connector, not only the cable diameter.

Conditional — verifyUse an open back bay below the drawer: This can work if the back still braces the case and dust access is acceptable. Retain a real racking path around the opening.

Stop and resolveDrill through the assembled back wherever the outlet lands: The hole may hit a slide, fastener, or unsupported veneer edge. Coordinate power before the case and drawer depths are released.

Decision challenge 3 of 3

When are drawer fronts sized?

Reveal depends on the square installed case, actual slide position, edge build-up, and neighboring openings.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathFit fronts after the drawer boxes run correctly in a square case: Fronts correct only the designed reveal, not a malfunctioning drawer. Prove motion first, then set visual gaps.

Conditional — verifyCut fronts to drawing size with trim allowance: This works when the case and edge system are highly repeatable. Preserve an intentional fitting allowance rather than guessing.

Stop and resolveUse oversized fronts to hide a racked case: The reveal may look acceptable while slides bind. Correct case geometry before cosmetic alignment.

After each reveal, record the verified condition in the brief and update every dependent dimension, material row, phase, and release check before continuing.

Six-phase path

Move from verified input to documented finish

Complete phases in the order shown unless the released project plan defines a safer dependency. Each checkbox represents a reviewed phase, not merely activity. Preserve the input, observed result, owner, date, revision, and exception record that lets another person reproduce why the work moved forward.

01

Phase 1 of 6 · 30–60 min

Measure

Outcome

Fit the nightstand to the made bed, user reach, outlet, and room movement.

Work sequence

  • Measure mattress and frame projections.
  • Mock up top height and footprint.
  • Trace outlet, cable, door, and drawer clearance.
Evidence to keep

Bedside elevation with reach and cable envelopes.

Hold point

Stop if the final mattress or slide system is unknown.

02

Phase 2 of 6 · 90–150 min

Plan

Outcome

Coordinate case, drawer, back, power route, base, and visible reveals.

Work sequence

  • Calculate the drawer opening from exact slide data.
  • Design the back and protected cable path.
  • Set top overhang, feet, and front reveals.
Evidence to keep

Released case section and hardware schedule.

Hold point

Stop if the plug cannot pass through or a drawer enters the cable zone.

03

Phase 3 of 6 · 3–4 hr

Cut

Outcome

Produce square case parts and one verified drawer set from actual material thickness.

Work sequence

  • Cut matched sides and square back.
  • Machine slide references from the bottom datum.
  • Cut one drawer and protected cable opening.
Evidence to keep

Labeled case and a passing drawer/slide first article.

Hold point

Stop if slide clearance or cable-edge protection differs from the plan.

04

Phase 4 of 6 · 3–4 hr

Assemble

Outcome

Build a square case, prove quiet drawer travel, and fit fronts without masking geometry errors.

Work sequence

  • Assemble and diagonal-check the case.
  • Install the back and verify cable access.
  • Run drawers repeatedly before fitting fronts.
Evidence to keep

Square case with smooth drawers and accessible cable path.

Hold point

Stop on slide bind, back racking, or a plug that cannot be removed.

05

Phase 5 of 6 · 20–40 min

Install

Outcome

Place the nightstand at the bed, protect the floor, and verify real reach and power use.

Work sequence

  • Install feet or glides.
  • Route an unplugged cable through the planned path.
  • Check drawer, bedding, baseboard, and vacuum clearance.
Evidence to keep

Final placement and cable-clearance record.

Hold point

Stop if the cord is pinched, the drawer contacts the bed, or the unit rocks.

06

Phase 6 of 6 · 30–45 min

Closeout

Outcome

Confirm nighttime function, finish cure, and hardware adjustment after the unit is loaded.

Work sequence

  • Load drawers and cycle them quietly.
  • Check lamp stability and top cleanup.
  • Record slides, finish, cable access, and care.
Evidence to keep

Loaded function test and maintenance note.

Hold point

Do not close until finish odor, rocking, or drawer drift is resolved.

Adapt without losing control

Useful variations and what they force you to revisit

A variation is not a cosmetic label. It can change the finished envelope, part set, sheet grouping, joinery, hardware, load, stability, transport, finish, installation, or removal path. Choose it early enough to update the authoritative project sources.

Open cubby plus shallow drawer

Use when: Use when books and a device need open reach while small personal items need one controlled drawer.

Carry through: The fixed cubby shelf becomes structural and its height must preserve both hand access and drawer hardware.

Side cable portal

Use when: Use when the outlet sits beside the case and a rear exit would pinch against the wall or baseboard.

Carry through: A protected side opening and visible removable clip keep the connector outside drawer and structural zones.

Adjustable-bed companion

Use when: Use after measuring mattress and frame positions across the bed's complete powered movement.

Carry through: Top height, gap, cable slack, and drawer pull must clear changing bed geometry rather than one static pose.

Catch rework while it is still a decision

Project risks, early signals, and hold actions

Use these risks as observation prompts rather than predictions. When a signal appears, protect the current condition, keep affected work visible, identify the smallest upstream cause, and repeat every dependent calculation, sample, fit, or inspection under the corrected revision.

Premature bedside height

Early signal: The top sits below bedding, above relaxed reach, or contacts the bed frame in use.

Response: Pause base or foot fabrication, mock up against the final made bed, and revise the finished support stack.

Drawer-cable collision

Early signal: The drawer rubs a cable, plug, grommet, or power brick near full travel.

Response: Disconnect power, shorten the drawer or relocate the protected cable route, then repeat full-travel testing.

Over-open back

Early signal: The case changes diagonal or sways after the cable access is cut.

Response: Restore a continuous rail, corner, or structural back path around a smaller serviceable opening.

Cosmetic front correction

Early signal: Front reveals appear even only while a drawer remains stiff, tilted, or partly closed.

Response: Remove the front, correct case square and slide datums, prove smooth loaded travel, then refit the reveal.

Never solve a mismatch by trimming or forcing the downstream part without recording what changed. A local adjustment can hide a wrong datum, wrong product, wrong material thickness, unsafe clearance, blocked assembly sequence, or installation conflict that will reappear later.

Finish line

Close the project against observable results

The nightstand works from the user's actual sleeping position and keeps drawer, power, back, and floor functions independent and serviceable.

  • Top height and reach are comfortable beside the made bed without contact through the bed's normal movement.
  • Each loaded drawer opens, disconnects, closes, and remains shut without audible cable contact or slide bind.
  • The case remains square and stable with the cable portal and removable back access installed.
  • The largest intended plug passes through the protected route and can be removed without disassembling the case.

Closeout should leave a usable trail for repair, repeat work, disassembly, warranty questions, and future changes. Save the released drawing or sketch, final parts and material records, product identities, approved exceptions, field changes, finish information, photographs where appropriate, and the final project-playbook CSV together.

Safety and authority boundaries

Use the playbook to expose uncertainty, not to override it

Follow current manufacturer instructions and use appropriate training, guarding, support, dust or fume control, ventilation, PPE, lifting methods, cure time, and disposal practices. Do not use this page as permission to operate unfamiliar equipment, defeat a safety system, exceed a tool or product rating, improvise a structural connection, or conceal work that has not passed the required inspection.

Structural, stair, guard, anchoring, seismic, electrical, plumbing, gas, fire, accessibility, and building-code decisions require the appropriate qualified professional or authority. Hardware clearances, fastener schedules, adhesive preparation, coating compatibility, load ratings, and installation methods come from the exact current product and project documentation. Verify them after any substitution.

Dimensions must name their datum, unit, finished or unfinished state, and measurement source. Check real openings at multiple locations; confirm square, level, plumb, flatness, obstructions, access, transport, assembly, service, and removal paths. Build a first article, sample, mockup, or dry fit when one failure could repeat across a batch or become expensive after finishing or installation.

Questions before starting

Project playbook FAQ

Can I build the nightstand project playbook exactly as shown?

No. This page is a planning playbook rather than a dimensioned drawing or approval. Verify the real site, design, loads, material, hardware, joinery, tooling, finish, installation, and applicable requirements before irreversible work.

Where is my project progress saved?

When browser storage is available, progress stays locally in this browser under a versioned WoodCutTool record. This page does not require an account and does not send the brief, choices, or phase status to a server.

What should I do when a phase cannot be completed?

Keep the affected work on hold. Identify whether the mismatch comes from the design, measurement, material, hardware, setup, site, sequence, or current instructions. Correct the controlling source and repeat dependent checks before continuing.

Which CSV should I download?

The static project-playbook.csv is a blank durable record of the published brief, materials, decisions, and six phases. The progress CSV is created in your browser and includes the values and completion state currently entered on the page.
