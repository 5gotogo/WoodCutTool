The planning promise

Know the next decision before material, time, or site access is committed

Finish with a box that sheds rain, dries after wet use, controls its lid in wind, and lets owners inspect the places where water and hardware usually fail first.

This playbook turns that promise into a controlled sequence: capture the project brief, verify material and hardware groups, work through three consequential choices, complete six observable phases, and close against a named finish line. It is designed to keep related resources together without pretending that a generic page knows the dimensions, loads, products, tools, site conditions, or requirements of a real job.

This is a planning playbook, not a dimensioned construction drawing, engineering review, code approval, machine instruction, or manufacturer authorization. Use it to organize questions and evidence. The released design, current product data, qualified review, field measurements, test pieces, and applicable requirements remain authoritative.

Private local working copy

Write the project brief before choosing a path

A useful brief records both the value and its source. Write the room, product, drawing, measurement method, responsible person, date, and revision where those details control the result. Entries remain in this browser when storage is available; they are not uploaded by this page.

Do not use a remembered dimension as a released input. If the source is missing, mark the field open and keep dependent purchasing, cutting, drilling, finishing, or installation on hold.

Choose, then reveal

Three decisions that change the project downstream

Select the condition that matches the verified brief before opening the consequence. This small pause prevents a familiar preference from masquerading as a project fact. The reveal explains what must be carried into dimensions, material, sequence, and evidence; it does not approve the choice.

Decision challenge 1 of 3

How should rain be managed at the lid?

A flush seam can admit wind-driven water even when the top looks flat and tight.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathUse slope, overhang, drip edges, and a drained ventilated inner boundary: Bulk water is shed before it reaches the storage seam. Layer water management instead of relying on one gasket.

Conditional — verifyUse a fitted weather-resistant cover over a ventilated box: This can protect seasonal storage if the cover is secured and removed for drying. Do not wrap wet contents in an airtight layer.

Stop and resolveCaulk the lid permanently around its frame: Service, ventilation, and moving joints become incompatible. A working lid cannot be made reliable through permanent sealing.

Decision challenge 2 of 3

How is the heavy lid controlled?

Wind and changing leverage can exceed a support that felt adequate near closed position.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathMatch paired supports and positive latches to measured lid mass and geometry: The lid opens, holds, closes, and stays shut through tested states. Use the final wet-finish and hardware weight.

Conditional — verifyDivide the lid into smaller independently controlled panels: This reduces moving mass when seams and access remain weather-managed. Test interaction when one panel is open and the other closed.

Stop and resolveUse a loose prop stick: The prop can slip while hands or a child are in the opening. Do not release an uncontrolled falling panel.

Decision challenge 3 of 3

Should wet items be placed directly inside?

Water brought in on toys, hoses, or tools can remain below dry cushions and against the floor.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathSeparate wet and dry zones with removable drained components: Wet gear can dry and the box can be inspected without unloading everything. Give water a shorter path out than through the stored contents.

Conditional — verifyRequire wet items to dry before storage: This works when the operating routine is realistic and the box remains ventilated. State the box's content boundary visibly.

Stop and resolveStore all gear in one sealed compartment: Condensation and introduced water remain hidden. Do not mix wet-entry gear with dry-sensitive storage without separation.

After each reveal, record the verified condition in the brief and update every dependent dimension, material row, phase, and release check before continuing.

Six-phase path

Move from verified input to documented finish

Complete phases in the order shown unless the released project plan defines a safer dependency. Each checkbox represents a reviewed phase, not merely activity. Preserve the input, observed result, owner, date, revision, and exception record that lets another person reproduce why the work moved forward.

01

Phase 1 of 6 · 60–90 min

Measure

Outcome

Define contents, wet-entry behavior, weather, lid clearance, base, runoff, and permission boundaries.

Work sequence

  • Measure contents and access opening.
  • Map rain, wind, sun, slope, and drain path.
  • Calculate final lid weight and movement.
Evidence to keep

Exposure and use brief with water and lid envelopes.

Hold point

Stop if runoff, anchoring, wind, or stored-content safety is unresolved.

02

Phase 2 of 6 · 2–3 hr

Plan

Outcome

Layer rain shedding, ventilation, drainage, lid control, raised support, and service access.

Work sequence

  • Draw lid slope, overhang, and drip path.
  • Separate wet and dry storage zones.
  • Specify feet, vents, supports, latches, and inspection panels.
Evidence to keep

Exploded water-management and lid-hardware plan.

Hold point

Stop if the design traps water beneath a fixed floor or behind an uninspectable liner.

03

Phase 3 of 6 · 3–5 hr plus finish

Cut

Outcome

Prepare exterior parts and samples before assembly hides wet edges and hardware mounting zones.

Work sequence

  • Cut shell, raised floor, and lid frame.
  • Seal end grain and machined edges.
  • Build full-weight lid support and water-edge mockups.
Evidence to keep

Passing lid-control and rain-edge samples.

Hold point

Stop on panel swelling, incompatible hardware, or unsupported lid fastener zones.

04

Phase 4 of 6 · 4–6 hr plus cure

Assemble

Outcome

Build a square, draining box and cycle the finished-weight lid before exposure.

Work sequence

  • Assemble shell with accessible joints.
  • Install removable floor, vents, lid, supports, and latches.
  • Run water and fifty-cycle lid tests.
Evidence to keep

Dry inspectable interior and stable controlled lid.

Hold point

Stop if water pools, supports drift, or latches miss after cycling.

05

Phase 5 of 6 · 45–90 min

Install

Outcome

Set the box above the surface with free drainage, airflow, lid travel, and permitted restraint.

Work sequence

  • Place and level replaceable feet.
  • Confirm runoff and cleaning access.
  • Apply only approved wind or anti-tip restraint.
Evidence to keep

Final placement, drain, lid, and restraint record.

Hold point

Stop if the box bridges a drain, rocks, or opens into a railing or wall.

06

Phase 6 of 6 · 45–75 min

Closeout

Outcome

Test weather path, wet/dry storage, lid safety, and routine inspection after loading.

Work sequence

  • Repeat a controlled rain-path test.
  • Load representative wet and dry contents separately.
  • Record latch, support, vent, finish, and foot maintenance.
Evidence to keep

Loaded weather and function test.

Hold point

Do not close with trapped water, uncontrolled lid travel, or inaccessible wet surfaces.

Adapt without losing control

Useful variations and what they force you to revisit

A variation is not a cosmetic label. It can change the finished envelope, part set, sheet grouping, joinery, hardware, load, stability, transport, finish, installation, or removal path. Choose it early enough to update the authoritative project sources.

Dry-bin cushion box

Use when: Use when removable lidded bins fit the complete cushion set and can be lifted through the lid opening.

Carry through: The shell can ventilate freely while bin handles, lid clearance, condensation inspection, and seasonal removal control capacity.

Drained garden-tool bay

Use when: Use when wet tools and hose fittings can stand in a separate removable floor zone away from dry contents.

Carry through: Drainage and abrasion-resistant liners enter the tool bay while sharp items need positive retention from the lid.

Two controlled lids

Use when: Use when separate storage zones benefit from smaller moving panels and each opening has independent hardware clearance.

Carry through: Individual lid mass falls, but seam water management, simultaneous-open stability, and paired latch maintenance increase.

Catch rework while it is still a decision

Project risks, early signals, and hold actions

Use these risks as observation prompts rather than predictions. When a signal appears, protect the current condition, keep affected work visible, identify the smallest upstream cause, and repeat every dependent calculation, sample, fit, or inspection under the corrected revision.

Flat-seam rain entry

Early signal: Water appears at the lid joint, hinge line, or upper wall during a directional spray test.

Response: Dry the box and revise slope, overhang, drip, gasket, or inner drainage before storing sensitive contents.

Support mismatch after finishing

Early signal: The finished lid falls, rises by itself, twists supports, or misses latches through its travel.

Response: Unload and restrain the lid, weigh or calculate the final assembly, and correct matched support geometry.

Wet-dry content mixing

Early signal: Cushions or dry gear show moisture transfer from tools, toys, hose, or the compartment floor.

Response: Remove contents, dry the box, and install a removable drained separation before returning dry-sensitive items.

Saturated bottom contact

Early signal: Feet, bottom edges, or fasteners remain wet or stained after surrounding runoff clears.

Response: Raise and isolate the box on replaceable supports, restore drainage and finish, and repeat the water test.

Never solve a mismatch by trimming or forcing the downstream part without recording what changed. A local adjustment can hide a wrong datum, wrong product, wrong material thickness, unsafe clearance, blocked assembly sequence, or installation conflict that will reappear later.

Finish line

Close the project against observable results

The outdoor box protects by shedding, separating, draining, and drying rather than by concealing water behind a heavy lid.

  • The controlled directional water test leaves the dry-storage zone dry and sends introduced water through the visible drain path.
  • The finished-weight lid completes repeated cycles, stays at the intended open angle, and latches closed without slam or twist.
  • Representative wet and dry contents remain separated and every liner, floor, vent, and drain can be removed or inspected.
  • All feet and lower edges clear standing water and remain visible after the box is placed on its final surface.

Closeout should leave a usable trail for repair, repeat work, disassembly, warranty questions, and future changes. Save the released drawing or sketch, final parts and material records, product identities, approved exceptions, field changes, finish information, photographs where appropriate, and the final project-playbook CSV together.

Safety and authority boundaries

Use the playbook to expose uncertainty, not to override it

Follow current manufacturer instructions and use appropriate training, guarding, support, dust or fume control, ventilation, PPE, lifting methods, cure time, and disposal practices. Do not use this page as permission to operate unfamiliar equipment, defeat a safety system, exceed a tool or product rating, improvise a structural connection, or conceal work that has not passed the required inspection.

Structural, stair, guard, anchoring, seismic, electrical, plumbing, gas, fire, accessibility, and building-code decisions require the appropriate qualified professional or authority. Hardware clearances, fastener schedules, adhesive preparation, coating compatibility, load ratings, and installation methods come from the exact current product and project documentation. Verify them after any substitution.

Dimensions must name their datum, unit, finished or unfinished state, and measurement source. Check real openings at multiple locations; confirm square, level, plumb, flatness, obstructions, access, transport, assembly, service, and removal paths. Build a first article, sample, mockup, or dry fit when one failure could repeat across a batch or become expensive after finishing or installation.

Questions before starting

Project playbook FAQ

Can I build the outdoor storage box project playbook exactly as shown?

No. This page is a planning playbook rather than a dimensioned drawing or approval. Verify the real site, design, loads, material, hardware, joinery, tooling, finish, installation, and applicable requirements before irreversible work.

Where is my project progress saved?

When browser storage is available, progress stays locally in this browser under a versioned WoodCutTool record. This page does not require an account and does not send the brief, choices, or phase status to a server.

What should I do when a phase cannot be completed?

Keep the affected work on hold. Identify whether the mismatch comes from the design, measurement, material, hardware, setup, site, sequence, or current instructions. Correct the controlling source and repeat dependent checks before continuing.

Which CSV should I download?

The static project-playbook.csv is a blank durable record of the published brief, materials, decisions, and six phases. The progress CSV is created in your browser and includes the values and completion state currently entered on the page.
