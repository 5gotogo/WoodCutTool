The planning promise

Know the next decision before material, time, or site access is committed

Finish with a planter that drains freely, protects vulnerable structure from direct wet-soil contact, and can be moved or repaired before water damage becomes hidden.

This playbook turns that promise into a controlled sequence: capture the project brief, verify material and hardware groups, work through three consequential choices, complete six observable phases, and close against a named finish line. It is designed to keep related resources together without pretending that a generic page knows the dimensions, loads, products, tools, site conditions, or requirements of a real job.

This is a planning playbook, not a dimensioned construction drawing, engineering review, code approval, machine instruction, or manufacturer authorization. Use it to organize questions and evidence. The released design, current product data, qualified review, field measurements, test pieces, and applicable requirements remain authoritative.

Private local working copy

Write the project brief before choosing a path

A useful brief records both the value and its source. Write the room, product, drawing, measurement method, responsible person, date, and revision where those details control the result. Entries remain in this browser when storage is available; they are not uploaded by this page.

Do not use a remembered dimension as a released input. If the source is missing, mark the field open and keep dependent purchasing, cutting, drilling, finishing, or installation on hold.

Material and hardware map

Verify purchasable groups, not just familiar names

The groups below are specification prompts, not a purchase order. Derive quantities from the released parts list, then reconcile actual stock size, measured thickness, face and grade requirements, grain direction, trim, kerf, defects, test pieces, hardware model, finish system, and supplier packaging before ordering.

Material or hardware groupSpecification to verify
Exterior shellDurable boards or exterior-rated panels appropriate to exposure and finish
Internal supportRails, cleats, or frames sized for wet-soil pressure and a supported false floor
Water-management layerRemovable liner or planting insert, filter fabric, drainage gaps, and outlet components
Exterior hardwareCorrosion-compatible fasteners, feet, brackets, and optional handles
Finish and isolationExterior finish, sealed end grain, and non-staining pads or replaceable feet

A substitution is a design change when it alters thickness, stiffness, machining, fastener capacity, edge treatment, finish compatibility, reveal, movement, load, or installation sequence. Record the substitution, identify every dependent part or phase, and issue a new revision instead of quietly carrying an old quantity forward.

Choose, then reveal

Three decisions that change the project downstream

Select the condition that matches the verified brief before opening the consequence. This small pause prevents a familiar preference from masquerading as a project fact. The reveal explains what must be carried into dimensions, material, sequence, and evidence; it does not approve the choice.

Decision challenge 1 of 3

Does soil contact the wooden shell?

Wet growing medium holds moisture and fertilizer against joints and hidden panel edges.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathUse a removable planting insert or drained liner separated from the shell: Water and soil can be serviced without making wood the permanent container. Separate horticultural containment from the visible furniture shell.

Conditional — verifyUse naturally durable boards with open drainage and replaceable interior parts: This accepts a finite service life when every wet surface can dry and be inspected. Plan replacement before fasteners and finish hide decay.

Stop and resolveFill a sealed plywood box directly with soil: Persistent moisture attacks edges, joints, and fasteners from inside. Do not make vulnerable panel core the planting liner.

Decision challenge 2 of 3

Where does excess water go?

Drainage that exists only as holes may still stain surfaces, rot feet, or affect spaces below.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathRoute water through a visible serviceable outlet to an approved destination: Flow can be tested, cleaned, and kept away from structural edges. Design the downstream water path, not just the exit hole.

Conditional — verifyUse a removable tray with defined overflow and inspection: This can suit small planters when capacity and emptying routine are realistic. Do not let the tray become a hidden reservoir.

Stop and resolveLet water find deck gaps: Runoff and staining consequences remain unknown. Stop before directing water onto shared or protected construction.

Decision challenge 3 of 3

Should the planter have casters?

Wet soil makes a compact planter far heavier and more top-heavy than its empty shell.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathUse a separately rated low rolling platform based on saturated load: Wheelbase, capacity, restraint, and replacement stay independent of the planter shell. Calculate movement from the wet condition.

Conditional — verifyUse fixed feet and move only when empty: This is simpler when seasonal handling can follow a documented emptying plan. Do not add decorative handles that imply loaded lifting.

Stop and resolveAdd small furniture casters after planting: Capacity, water exposure, and tipping behavior are unverified. Mobility is a loaded structural system.

After each reveal, record the verified condition in the brief and update every dependent dimension, material row, phase, and release check before continuing.

Six-phase path

Move from verified input to documented finish

Complete phases in the order shown unless the released project plan defines a safer dependency. Each checkbox represents a reviewed phase, not merely activity. Preserve the input, observed result, owner, date, revision, and exception record that lets another person reproduce why the work moved forward.

01

Phase 1 of 6 · 45–75 min

Measure

Outcome

Define plant needs, saturated load, exposure, drain destination, and surface limits.

Work sequence

  • Size mature root and soil volume.
  • Estimate wet mass and center of gravity.
  • Trace drainage and staining boundaries.
Evidence to keep

Planting, load, and water-path brief.

Hold point

Stop if runoff destination or supporting surface capacity is uncertain.

02

Phase 2 of 6 · 90–150 min

Plan

Outcome

Separate structural shell, planting containment, drainage, feet, and replaceable weather parts.

Work sequence

  • Design shell and wet-load supports.
  • Choose removable liner or insert.
  • Place drain, ventilation, isolation, and service access.
Evidence to keep

Section showing soil, water, structure, and repair paths.

Hold point

Stop if water must cross an unsealed structural joint to exit.

03

Phase 3 of 6 · 2–4 hr plus finish

Cut

Outcome

Prepare exterior components and protect end grain, holes, and edges before assembly.

Work sequence

  • Cut shell and support parts.
  • Slope or gap the supported false floor.
  • Make finish and liner-interface samples.
Evidence to keep

Protected parts and passing liner/drain sample.

Hold point

Stop on incompatible fasteners, trapped ledges, or inaccessible raw edges.

04

Phase 4 of 6 · 2–4 hr plus cure

Assemble

Outcome

Build a square shell and prove support and drainage without soil hiding the work.

Work sequence

  • Assemble shell and structural base.
  • Install liner or planting insert and outlet.
  • Water-test drainage and inspect every joint.
Evidence to keep

Empty planter that drains completely and remains inspectable.

Hold point

Stop if water pools, leaks into panel core, or exits onto an unapproved surface.

05

Phase 5 of 6 · 30–60 min

Install

Outcome

Place and level the planter so feet, drainage, access, and wind behavior match the surveyed location.

Work sequence

  • Install isolation feet or rated platform.
  • Level without blocking the drain.
  • Confirm clearance from walls, railings, and service paths.
Evidence to keep

Final placement and drainage-clearance record.

Hold point

Stop if the planter blocks access or transfers load to an unsuitable edge or railing.

06

Phase 6 of 6 · 45–75 min

Closeout

Outcome

Load soil in stages, retest drainage, and record seasonal inspection and repair points.

Work sequence

  • Add liner, medium, and irrigation progressively.
  • Run a full watering test.
  • Record wet-load limit, outlet cleaning, finish, and foot inspection.
Evidence to keep

Loaded water test and maintenance schedule.

Hold point

Do not close while the shell bulges, water backs up, or supporting feet settle.

Adapt without losing control

Useful variations and what they force you to revisit

A variation is not a cosmetic label. It can change the finished envelope, part set, sheet grouping, joinery, hardware, load, stability, transport, finish, installation, or removal path. Choose it early enough to update the authoritative project sources.

Slatted shell with nursery pots

Use when: Use when standard removable containers provide the required root volume and can be lifted safely when serviced.

Carry through: The shell stays ventilated and soil-free, while pot support, visual gaps, irrigation capture, and replacement sizes drive the frame.

Long herb trough

Use when: Use when shallow-root crops, irrigation flow, and one approved drain destination support a narrow continuous bed.

Carry through: A serviceable drain manifold and intermediate wet-load supports prevent one blocked outlet or long side from controlling failure.

Separately rolling planter

Use when: Use only when a low platform is rated for the complete saturated mass and the surface route is level and unobstructed.

Carry through: Mobility remains replaceable beneath the shell, but wheelbase, brakes, steering, runoff, and no-passenger rules need closeout.

Catch rework while it is still a decision

Project risks, early signals, and hold actions

Use these risks as observation prompts rather than predictions. When a signal appears, protect the current condition, keep affected work visible, identify the smallest upstream cause, and repeat every dependent calculation, sample, fit, or inspection under the corrected revision.

Dry-weight design

Early signal: Feet, side panels, handles, or casters deflect as soil is watered toward saturation.

Response: Stop filling or moving, unload safely, and redesign from the declared saturated medium, water, shell, and plant mass.

Uncontrolled runoff

Early signal: Water reaches a wall, neighbor, deck edge, staining surface, or area outside the approved drain destination.

Response: Stop irrigation, contain the discharge, and reroute through a visible serviceable outlet before reloading the planter.

Wet liner contact

Early signal: Moisture remains trapped between liner and unsealed shell edge after the drainage test.

Response: Remove the liner, dry and repair the edge, then add separation and ventilation before soil installation.

Hardware-material reaction

Early signal: Fasteners or wood develop streaking, corrosion, loosening, or discoloration at wet joints.

Response: Quarantine the connection and replace it with a compatible documented material after inspecting surrounding wood.

Never solve a mismatch by trimming or forcing the downstream part without recording what changed. A local adjustment can hide a wrong datum, wrong product, wrong material thickness, unsafe clearance, blocked assembly sequence, or installation conflict that will reappear later.

Finish line

Close the project against observable results

The planter behaves as a water-managed growing container and a repairable outdoor shell, with saturated load and discharge visible rather than hidden.

  • The filled and fully watered planter shows no persistent side bow, foot settlement, joint opening, or support movement.
  • Test water reaches only the documented outlet or removable tray and no moisture remains against vulnerable shell edges.
  • The planting insert, liner, tray, or outlet can be removed and cleaned without dismantling structural sides.
  • Feet, fasteners, finish edges, and the downstream surface remain inspectable after the final irrigation test.

Closeout should leave a usable trail for repair, repeat work, disassembly, warranty questions, and future changes. Save the released drawing or sketch, final parts and material records, product identities, approved exceptions, field changes, finish information, photographs where appropriate, and the final project-playbook CSV together.

Safety and authority boundaries

Use the playbook to expose uncertainty, not to override it

Follow current manufacturer instructions and use appropriate training, guarding, support, dust or fume control, ventilation, PPE, lifting methods, cure time, and disposal practices. Do not use this page as permission to operate unfamiliar equipment, defeat a safety system, exceed a tool or product rating, improvise a structural connection, or conceal work that has not passed the required inspection.

Structural, stair, guard, anchoring, seismic, electrical, plumbing, gas, fire, accessibility, and building-code decisions require the appropriate qualified professional or authority. Hardware clearances, fastener schedules, adhesive preparation, coating compatibility, load ratings, and installation methods come from the exact current product and project documentation. Verify them after any substitution.

Dimensions must name their datum, unit, finished or unfinished state, and measurement source. Check real openings at multiple locations; confirm square, level, plumb, flatness, obstructions, access, transport, assembly, service, and removal paths. Build a first article, sample, mockup, or dry fit when one failure could repeat across a batch or become expensive after finishing or installation.

Questions before starting

Project playbook FAQ

Can I build the planter box project playbook exactly as shown?

No. This page is a planning playbook rather than a dimensioned drawing or approval. Verify the real site, design, loads, material, hardware, joinery, tooling, finish, installation, and applicable requirements before irreversible work.

Where is my project progress saved?

When browser storage is available, progress stays locally in this browser under a versioned WoodCutTool record. This page does not require an account and does not send the brief, choices, or phase status to a server.

What should I do when a phase cannot be completed?

Keep the affected work on hold. Identify whether the mismatch comes from the design, measurement, material, hardware, setup, site, sequence, or current instructions. Correct the controlling source and repeat dependent checks before continuing.

Which CSV should I download?

The static project-playbook.csv is a blank durable record of the published brief, materials, decisions, and six phases. The progress CSV is created in your browser and includes the values and completion state currently entered on the page.
