The planning promise

Know the next decision before material, time, or site access is committed

Finish with a bed that gardeners can reach without stepping into soil, that drains without losing its mix, and whose sides and corners resist the declared wet-soil load.

This playbook turns that promise into a controlled sequence: capture the project brief, verify material and hardware groups, work through three consequential choices, complete six observable phases, and close against a named finish line. It is designed to keep related resources together without pretending that a generic page knows the dimensions, loads, products, tools, site conditions, or requirements of a real job.

This is a planning playbook, not a dimensioned construction drawing, engineering review, code approval, machine instruction, or manufacturer authorization. Use it to organize questions and evidence. The released design, current product data, qualified review, field measurements, test pieces, and applicable requirements remain authoritative.

Private local working copy

Write the project brief before choosing a path

A useful brief records both the value and its source. Write the room, product, drawing, measurement method, responsible person, date, and revision where those details control the result. Entries remain in this browser when storage is available; they are not uploaded by this page.

Do not use a remembered dimension as a released input. If the source is missing, mark the field open and keep dependent purchasing, cutting, drilling, finishing, or installation on hold.

Material and hardware map

Verify purchasable groups, not just familiar names

The groups below are specification prompts, not a purchase order. Derive quantities from the released parts list, then reconcile actual stock size, measured thickness, face and grade requirements, grain direction, trim, kerf, defects, test pieces, hardware model, finish system, and supplier packaging before ordering.

Material or hardware groupSpecification to verify
Bed sidesDurable suitable boards or approved material sized for span, height, and wet-soil exposure
Corner and mid-span structurePosts, cleats, stakes, or external braces with serviceable connections
Corrosion-compatible fastenersExterior screws or bolts selected for the wood treatment and soil environment
Drainage and soil-retention layerOpen base, optional mesh where appropriate, and fabric used only without blocking drainage
Irrigation and edge detailsTubing supports, cap rails, trellis blocking, and replaceable contact strips

A substitution is a design change when it alters thickness, stiffness, machining, fastener capacity, edge treatment, finish compatibility, reveal, movement, load, or installation sequence. Record the substitution, identify every dependent part or phase, and issue a new revision instead of quietly carrying an old quantity forward.

Choose, then reveal

Three decisions that change the project downstream

Select the condition that matches the verified brief before opening the consequence. This small pause prevents a familiar preference from masquerading as a project fact. The reveal explains what must be carried into dimensions, material, sequence, and evidence; it does not approve the choice.

Decision challenge 1 of 3

How wide should the bed be?

A generous planting area fails when the center requires stepping into and compacting the soil.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathSet width from tested reach on every accessible side: The gardener reaches the center while paths remain outside the soil. Mock up reach at the final bed height, not on a low table.

Conditional — verifyUse a wider bed with a deliberate central access path: This can work when the path does not interrupt drainage or root zones. Count the access path in soil volume and structure.

Stop and resolveUse four feet wide against a wall: The rear half may be inaccessible for planting and harvest. One-sided beds must be sized from one-sided reach.

Decision challenge 2 of 3

What resists wet-soil pressure?

Side force increases with soil depth, water, span, and loss of connection stiffness over time.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathUse corners and intermediate braces sized for the declared height and span: Long sides have a visible path into replaceable structural members. Treat wet soil as a sustained lateral load.

Conditional — verifyReduce bed height or side-bay length: Smaller loaded panels can simplify bracing while preserving useful root volume. Recalculate soil and crop program after resizing.

Stop and resolveAdd soil first and brace if the boards bow: Movement damages joints and makes a loaded repair difficult. Release structure before the irreversible bulk fill.

Decision challenge 3 of 3

Should the bottom be lined?

A blanket liner can block drainage, trap roots, or create a perched water layer.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathKeep an open draining base and use only purpose-specific permeable barriers: Water and roots follow a declared site-appropriate boundary. Choose mesh or fabric for a named problem, not as a default layer.

Conditional — verifyUse a raised contained floor only for a designed accessible planter: This can work when the floor and drainage carry the full saturated load. Treat it as a structural planter, not a ground bed.

Stop and resolveInstall an impermeable sheet without an engineered outlet: Water can collect unseen and overload or drown the bed. Do not create a basin accidentally.

After each reveal, record the verified condition in the brief and update every dependent dimension, material row, phase, and release check before continuing.

Six-phase path

Move from verified input to documented finish

Complete phases in the order shown unless the released project plan defines a safer dependency. Each checkbox represents a reviewed phase, not merely activity. Preserve the input, observed result, owner, date, revision, and exception record that lets another person reproduce why the work moved forward.

01

Phase 1 of 6 · 60–90 min

Measure

Outcome

Verify crop needs, utilities, sun, slope, drainage, reach, paths, and saturated soil volume.

Work sequence

  • Complete site and utility checks.
  • Mock up bed height, width, and reach.
  • Calculate soil volume and wet-load basis.
Evidence to keep

Site plan and garden-use/load brief.

Hold point

Stop if utilities, runoff, property rules, or supporting ground are unresolved.

02

Phase 2 of 6 · 2–3 hr

Plan

Outcome

Divide the bed into reachable, braced, draining bays with accessible irrigation and paths.

Work sequence

  • Set side spans, corners, and braces.
  • Choose compatible material and fasteners.
  • Lay out irrigation, trellis blocking, and soil delivery.
Evidence to keep

Bed plan with structural bays, paths, and fill sequence.

Hold point

Stop if soil delivery would arrive before the empty structure can be inspected.

03

Phase 3 of 6 · 3–4 hr

Cut

Outcome

Prepare durable side, corner, brace, and cap parts with protected ends and repeatable bay lengths.

Work sequence

  • Cut sides and braces from one length schedule.
  • Pre-drill serviceable connections.
  • Make a ground-contact and fastener compatibility sample.
Evidence to keep

Labeled parts and approved connection/material sample.

Hold point

Stop on unsuitable treated material, split ends, weak edge distance, or incompatible metal.

04

Phase 4 of 6 · 3–5 hr

Assemble

Outcome

Build a square empty bed and prove every brace and irrigation route before soil conceals it.

Work sequence

  • Assemble corner frames and side bays.
  • Square from diagonals and install mid-span braces.
  • Dry-route irrigation and any purposeful barrier.
Evidence to keep

Square empty bed with complete structural and drainage inspection.

Hold point

Stop if sides move under hand pressure or drainage fabric bridges outlets.

05

Phase 5 of 6 · 2–4 hr

Install

Outcome

Place, level, and stabilize the bed while preserving site drainage and path geometry.

Work sequence

  • Prepare the approved ground surface.
  • Set bed without trapping runoff.
  • Verify paths, trellis side, irrigation supply, and delivery access.
Evidence to keep

Final site, path, level, and drainage record before fill.

Hold point

Stop if leveling sends runoff toward a structure or path width is lost.

06

Phase 6 of 6 · 2–4 hr plus soil handling

Closeout

Outcome

Fill in controlled lifts, observe side movement and drainage, and retain maintenance access.

Work sequence

  • Add soil in even lifts while monitoring sides.
  • Run irrigation and drainage tests.
  • Record soil volume, brace movement, seasonal checks, and repair access.
Evidence to keep

Filled-bed inspection with before/after side measurements.

Hold point

Do not close after bowing, joint movement, standing water, or path settlement.

Adapt without losing control

Useful variations and what they force you to revisit

A variation is not a cosmetic label. It can change the finished envelope, part set, sheet grouping, joinery, hardware, load, stability, transport, finish, installation, or removal path. Choose it early enough to update the authoritative project sources.

Two-sided vegetable bed

Use when: Use when paths remain available along both long sides and the tested reach meets at the center without stepping in soil.

Carry through: A wider productive zone is possible, while irrigation, trellis orientation, and mid-span braces must remain reachable from either side.

One-sided fence bed

Use when: Use only when a narrower tested reach serves the rear crop row and the fence retains required inspection access.

Carry through: Width decreases and rear fasteners, weeds, irrigation, and trellis work need a planned front-access sequence.

Accessible contained planter

Use when: Use when a qualified structural design provides a supported floor, drainage, and approach clearance above ground.

Carry through: It is no longer a simple open-bottom bed; saturated-floor load, outlet maintenance, and accessibility geometry govern the build.

Catch rework while it is still a decision

Project risks, early signals, and hold actions

Use these risks as observation prompts rather than predictions. When a signal appears, protect the current condition, keep affected work visible, identify the smallest upstream cause, and repeat every dependent calculation, sample, fit, or inspection under the corrected revision.

Unreachable center soil

Early signal: Planting, weeding, or harvest requires stepping in soil or leaning beyond the approved reach mockup.

Response: Reduce bed width or add a deliberate non-soil access path before filling and planting the affected bay.

Long-side soil bow

Early signal: Side boards move outward, brace gaps open, or corner fasteners rotate during staged fill or watering.

Response: Stop filling, unload the affected lift safely, and add or redesign corner and intermediate restraint before resuming.

Accidental water basin

Early signal: Water stands above an impermeable layer or drains only through an unverified puncture after irrigation.

Response: Remove fill as needed, restore a purposeful permeable or engineered outlet path, and retest empty before refill.

Soil arrives before release

Early signal: Bulk delivery blocks paths or pressures the team to fill before square, brace, utility, and drainage checks pass.

Response: Hold or relocate delivery and complete the empty-bed inspection; schedule pressure cannot substitute for structural evidence.

Never solve a mismatch by trimming or forcing the downstream part without recording what changed. A local adjustment can hide a wrong datum, wrong product, wrong material thickness, unsafe clearance, blocked assembly sequence, or installation conflict that will reappear later.

Finish line

Close the project against observable results

The raised bed remains a reachable, draining garden after saturated soil is added, with observable structure and repair access around every loaded bay.

  • Every planting point can be reached from the approved path without stepping into soil or loading the side wall.
  • Before-and-after measurements show no continuing side bow, corner opening, brace movement, or path settlement after watering.
  • A full irrigation test drains through the intended ground or engineered outlet without standing water against side members.
  • Paths, irrigation valves, trellis connections, braces, and replaceable side fasteners remain accessible after final fill.

Closeout should leave a usable trail for repair, repeat work, disassembly, warranty questions, and future changes. Save the released drawing or sketch, final parts and material records, product identities, approved exceptions, field changes, finish information, photographs where appropriate, and the final project-playbook CSV together.

Safety and authority boundaries

Use the playbook to expose uncertainty, not to override it

Follow current manufacturer instructions and use appropriate training, guarding, support, dust or fume control, ventilation, PPE, lifting methods, cure time, and disposal practices. Do not use this page as permission to operate unfamiliar equipment, defeat a safety system, exceed a tool or product rating, improvise a structural connection, or conceal work that has not passed the required inspection.

Structural, stair, guard, anchoring, seismic, electrical, plumbing, gas, fire, accessibility, and building-code decisions require the appropriate qualified professional or authority. Hardware clearances, fastener schedules, adhesive preparation, coating compatibility, load ratings, and installation methods come from the exact current product and project documentation. Verify them after any substitution.

Dimensions must name their datum, unit, finished or unfinished state, and measurement source. Check real openings at multiple locations; confirm square, level, plumb, flatness, obstructions, access, transport, assembly, service, and removal paths. Build a first article, sample, mockup, or dry fit when one failure could repeat across a batch or become expensive after finishing or installation.

Questions before starting

Project playbook FAQ

Can I build the raised garden bed project playbook exactly as shown?

No. This page is a planning playbook rather than a dimensioned drawing or approval. Verify the real site, design, loads, material, hardware, joinery, tooling, finish, installation, and applicable requirements before irreversible work.

Where is my project progress saved?

When browser storage is available, progress stays locally in this browser under a versioned WoodCutTool record. This page does not require an account and does not send the brief, choices, or phase status to a server.

What should I do when a phase cannot be completed?

Keep the affected work on hold. Identify whether the mismatch comes from the design, measurement, material, hardware, setup, site, sequence, or current instructions. Correct the controlling source and repeat dependent checks before continuing.

Which CSV should I download?

The static project-playbook.csv is a blank durable record of the published brief, materials, decisions, and six phases. The progress CSV is created in your browser and includes the values and completion state currently entered on the page.
