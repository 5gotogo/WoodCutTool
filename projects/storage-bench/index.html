The planning promise

Know the next decision before material, time, or site access is committed

Finish with a bench that feels stable as a seat, opens without trapping fingers, and stores the intended items without blocking hinges or ventilation.

This playbook turns that promise into a controlled sequence: capture the project brief, verify material and hardware groups, work through three consequential choices, complete six observable phases, and close against a named finish line. It is designed to keep related resources together without pretending that a generic page knows the dimensions, loads, products, tools, site conditions, or requirements of a real job.

This is a planning playbook, not a dimensioned construction drawing, engineering review, code approval, machine instruction, or manufacturer authorization. Use it to organize questions and evidence. The released design, current product data, qualified review, field measurements, test pieces, and applicable requirements remain authoritative.

Private local working copy

Write the project brief before choosing a path

A useful brief records both the value and its source. Write the room, product, drawing, measurement method, responsible person, date, and revision where those details control the result. Entries remain in this browser when storage is available; they are not uploaded by this page.

Do not use a remembered dimension as a released input. If the source is missing, mark the field open and keep dependent purchasing, cutting, drilling, finishing, or installation on hold.

Material and hardware map

Verify purchasable groups, not just familiar names

The groups below are specification prompts, not a purchase order. Derive quantities from the released parts list, then reconcile actual stock size, measured thickness, face and grade requirements, grain direction, trim, kerf, defects, test pieces, hardware model, finish system, and supplier packaging before ordering.

A substitution is a design change when it alters thickness, stiffness, machining, fastener capacity, edge treatment, finish compatibility, reveal, movement, load, or installation sequence. Record the substitution, identify every dependent part or phase, and issue a new revision instead of quietly carrying an old quantity forward.

Choose, then reveal

Three decisions that change the project downstream

Select the condition that matches the verified brief before opening the consequence. This small pause prevents a familiar preference from masquerading as a project fact. The reveal explains what must be carried into dimensions, material, sequence, and evidence; it does not approve the choice.

Decision challenge 1 of 3

How will the lid be controlled through its full travel?

A heavy lid changes leverage as it opens and can pinch fingers or strike the wall.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathUse tested lid supports matched to weight and geometry: Opening effort, hold-open behavior, and closing speed are verified with the finished lid. Test the exact lid, not only the hinge catalog claim.

Conditional — verifyUse a divided or lighter lid: Reduced moving mass can improve safety if each section still has controlled travel. Check the center seam, child access, and hardware interference.

Stop and resolveRely on friction hinges without a weight check: The lid may fall as hardware wears or geometry changes. Do not treat uncertain friction as a safety control.

Decision challenge 2 of 3

What carries the seated load?

The top panel alone should not bridge a long storage opening without a defined support path.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathTransfer load through ends, dividers, and a front/back rail system: The seat is supported at useful intervals and the box resists racking. Place structure where it does not block the storage program.

Conditional — verifyAdd a centered divider under the longest span: This can suit basket storage when the divider aligns with the load and joinery. Verify that the remaining openings still fit their contents.

Stop and resolveIncrease finish thickness on an unsupported lid: Surface coating does not create a reliable seating structure. Resolve the span and connections, not the appearance.

Decision challenge 3 of 3

Should the bench be restrained?

Tall backs, open lids, children climbing, and uneven floors can shift the center of mass.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathEvaluate tip and slide behavior, then anchor where needed: Restraint is tied to real use and verified structure. Test the worst open-lid and edge-loading condition before release.

Conditional — verifyUse a wide plinth and non-slip feet for a low freestanding bench: This can work when the documented stability test passes. Freestanding does not mean untested.

Stop and resolveAssume stored weight prevents movement: Contents change and may increase injury when the unit tips. Do not use unknown future contents as restraint.

After each reveal, record the verified condition in the brief and update every dependent dimension, material row, phase, and release check before continuing.

Six-phase path

Move from verified input to documented finish

Complete phases in the order shown unless the released project plan defines a safer dependency. Each checkbox represents a reviewed phase, not merely activity. Preserve the input, observed result, owner, date, revision, and exception record that lets another person reproduce why the work moved forward.

01

Phase 1 of 6 · 45–75 min

Measure

Outcome

Fit the bench to users, contents, room surfaces, and the complete lid movement.

Work sequence

  • Measure finished seat height and footprint.
  • Size baskets or stored objects.
  • Map wall, floor, baseboard, vent, and lid clearances.
Evidence to keep

Room and lid-envelope sketch with load and storage dimensions.

Hold point

Stop if the lid hardware or largest stored item is not known.

02

Phase 2 of 6 · 2–3 hr

Plan

Outcome

Build a structural path around usable storage and controlled moving hardware.

Work sequence

  • Set divider and rail locations from seat span.
  • Model hinge and lid-support geometry.
  • Choose base, ventilation, and restraint details.
Evidence to keep

Sectioned bench plan showing structure, storage, lid, and restraint.

Hold point

Stop if hardware screws land in weak edges or a divider blocks the intended contents.

03

Phase 3 of 6 · 3–4 hr

Cut

Outcome

Produce paired structural panels, a flat lid, and accurate hardware reference pieces.

Work sequence

  • Cut and label ends, dividers, rails, and base.
  • Prepare the lid with finished edge allowances.
  • Make a full-scale hinge and support mockup.
Evidence to keep

Passing lid-hardware mockup and measured structural parts.

Hold point

Stop if the lid is warped or the hardware cannot reach its rated geometry.

04

Phase 4 of 6 · 3–5 hr

Assemble

Outcome

Create a square, rack-resistant bench and test the lid at finished weight.

Work sequence

  • Assemble base, ends, dividers, and rails.
  • Fit the lid with bumpers and finger clearances.
  • Add equivalent finish or cushion weight for the function test.
Evidence to keep

Stable box and controlled lid that holds and closes as intended.

Hold point

Stop if any support detaches, binds, or slams during repeated cycles.

05

Phase 5 of 6 · 60–120 min

Install

Outcome

Level and place the bench without blocking air, doors, or cleaning access.

Work sequence

  • Fit the plinth around baseboard or floor variation.
  • Verify clear lid travel in the final location.
  • Install any required wall restraint into verified structure.
Evidence to keep

Final placement record with level, clearance, and restraint evidence.

Hold point

Stop if placement creates a pinch point or covers a required vent or service.

06

Phase 6 of 6 · 30–60 min

Closeout

Outcome

Prove seating, storage, lid control, and routine cleaning under normal use.

Work sequence

  • Apply a gradual distributed seat-load test.
  • Cycle the lid repeatedly with contents installed.
  • Record hardware model, adjustment, and inspection interval.
Evidence to keep

Function test and owner-facing load and lid-care note.

Hold point

Do not close while the bench rocks, lid behavior changes, or restraint loosens.

Adapt without losing control

Useful variations and what they force you to revisit

A variation is not a cosmetic label. It can change the finished envelope, part set, sheet grouping, joinery, hardware, load, stability, transport, finish, installation, or removal path. Choose it early enough to update the authoritative project sources.

Vented shoe bench

Use when: Use when baskets or open bays can separate wet footwear from the structural seat and room surfaces.

Carry through: Vent area, drip control, basket retrieval, and lower-edge finish replace a sealed storage floor.

Divided blanket lids

Use when: Use when two smaller openings serve the contents and each lid has its own controlled support.

Carry through: Moving mass decreases, but the center seam, paired hinges, simultaneous-open stability, and cushion split need testing.

Mudroom back panel

Use when: Use only when the tall coat or hook panel has an independent attachment path into verified wall structure.

Carry through: The bench remains seating, while hanging and climbing loads bypass the lid and shallow storage box.

Catch rework while it is still a decision

Project risks, early signals, and hold actions

Use these risks as observation prompts rather than predictions. When a signal appears, protect the current condition, keep affected work visible, identify the smallest upstream cause, and repeat every dependent calculation, sample, fit, or inspection under the corrected revision.

Mismatched lid supports

Early signal: The finished lid drops, accelerates, refuses to close, or twists one support before the other.

Response: Unload the lid, verify final mass and mounting geometry, then select and reposition matched hardware from current data.

Unsupported seat span

Early signal: The seat visibly deflects, joints open, or the lid rubs when load is applied between dividers.

Response: Remove use load and add a designed rail, divider, or shorter span before repeating the staged test.

Open-lid instability

Early signal: The bench slides, rocks, or tips toward the wall as the lid reaches its hold-open angle.

Response: Stop use, revise feet or restraint and lid geometry, then retest with the compartment empty and loaded.

Blocked room ventilation

Early signal: The plinth covers a heater or return path, or warm air concentrates inside storage.

Response: Move or redesign the bench around the verified service clearance; do not cut an improvised vent without system review.

Never solve a mismatch by trimming or forcing the downstream part without recording what changed. A local adjustment can hide a wrong datum, wrong product, wrong material thickness, unsafe clearance, blocked assembly sequence, or installation conflict that will reappear later.

Finish line

Close the project against observable results

The storage bench behaves as seating, moving-lid furniture, and storage at the same time without one role defeating another.

  • The seat holds the declared distributed and edge-load tests without rocking, persistent deflection, or opening joints.
  • The finished-weight lid opens, holds, and closes repeatedly without slamming or exposing a finger pinch.
  • Every named basket or stored item can be removed without contacting hinges, supports, or the seat frame.
  • The installed bench preserves room ventilation, lid clearance, cleaning access, and any required restraint.

Closeout should leave a usable trail for repair, repeat work, disassembly, warranty questions, and future changes. Save the released drawing or sketch, final parts and material records, product identities, approved exceptions, field changes, finish information, photographs where appropriate, and the final project-playbook CSV together.

Safety and authority boundaries

Use the playbook to expose uncertainty, not to override it

Follow current manufacturer instructions and use appropriate training, guarding, support, dust or fume control, ventilation, PPE, lifting methods, cure time, and disposal practices. Do not use this page as permission to operate unfamiliar equipment, defeat a safety system, exceed a tool or product rating, improvise a structural connection, or conceal work that has not passed the required inspection.

Structural, stair, guard, anchoring, seismic, electrical, plumbing, gas, fire, accessibility, and building-code decisions require the appropriate qualified professional or authority. Hardware clearances, fastener schedules, adhesive preparation, coating compatibility, load ratings, and installation methods come from the exact current product and project documentation. Verify them after any substitution.

Dimensions must name their datum, unit, finished or unfinished state, and measurement source. Check real openings at multiple locations; confirm square, level, plumb, flatness, obstructions, access, transport, assembly, service, and removal paths. Build a first article, sample, mockup, or dry fit when one failure could repeat across a batch or become expensive after finishing or installation.

Questions before starting

Project playbook FAQ

Can I build the storage bench project playbook exactly as shown?

No. This page is a planning playbook rather than a dimensioned drawing or approval. Verify the real site, design, loads, material, hardware, joinery, tooling, finish, installation, and applicable requirements before irreversible work.

Where is my project progress saved?

When browser storage is available, progress stays locally in this browser under a versioned WoodCutTool record. This page does not require an account and does not send the brief, choices, or phase status to a server.

What should I do when a phase cannot be completed?

Keep the affected work on hold. Identify whether the mismatch comes from the design, measurement, material, hardware, setup, site, sequence, or current instructions. Correct the controlling source and repeat dependent checks before continuing.

Which CSV should I download?

The static project-playbook.csv is a blank durable record of the published brief, materials, decisions, and six phases. The progress CSV is created in your browser and includes the values and completion state currently entered on the page.
