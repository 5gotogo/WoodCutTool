The planning promise

Know the next decision before material, time, or site access is committed

Finish with a square cabinet whose hanging method, clearances, and working height were verified before the loaded box reaches the wall.

This playbook turns that promise into a controlled sequence: capture the project brief, verify material and hardware groups, work through three consequential choices, complete six observable phases, and close against a named finish line. It is designed to keep related resources together without pretending that a generic page knows the dimensions, loads, products, tools, site conditions, or requirements of a real job.

This is a planning playbook, not a dimensioned construction drawing, engineering review, code approval, machine instruction, or manufacturer authorization. Use it to organize questions and evidence. The released design, current product data, qualified review, field measurements, test pieces, and applicable requirements remain authoritative.

Private local working copy

Write the project brief before choosing a path

A useful brief records both the value and its source. Write the room, product, drawing, measurement method, responsible person, date, and revision where those details control the result. Entries remain in this browser when storage is available; they are not uploaded by this page.

Do not use a remembered dimension as a released input. If the source is missing, mark the field open and keep dependent purchasing, cutting, drilling, finishing, or installation on hold.

Material and hardware map

Verify purchasable groups, not just familiar names

The groups below are specification prompts, not a purchase order. Derive quantities from the released parts list, then reconcile actual stock size, measured thickness, face and grade requirements, grain direction, trim, kerf, defects, test pieces, hardware model, finish system, and supplier packaging before ordering.

Material or hardware groupSpecification to verify
Cabinet plywood3/4 in panels for sides, tops, bottoms, and shelves
Structural back or railsBack panel, nailers, or tested hanging-rail components sized for the design
Doors and edgesDoor stock, matching edge treatment, bumpers, and confirmed hinges
Attachment systemFasteners and brackets selected for verified wall structure and cabinet load

A substitution is a design change when it alters thickness, stiffness, machining, fastener capacity, edge treatment, finish compatibility, reveal, movement, load, or installation sequence. Record the substitution, identify every dependent part or phase, and issue a new revision instead of quietly carrying an old quantity forward.

Choose, then reveal

Three decisions that change the project downstream

Select the condition that matches the verified brief before opening the consequence. This small pause prevents a familiar preference from masquerading as a project fact. The reveal explains what must be carried into dimensions, material, sequence, and evidence; it does not approve the choice.

Decision challenge 1 of 3

What carries the cabinet load into the wall?

The visible box is only as reliable as the continuous load path through its back or rails and into known structure.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathDesigned hanging rails into verified structure: The load path is inspectable and installation can be leveled before final fastening. Design attachment as part of the cabinet, not an installation afterthought.

Conditional — verifyFull structural back with distributed fastening: This works when back thickness, joinery, fasteners, and wall structure are all specified. Verify the back-to-box connection as carefully as the wall fasteners.

Stop and resolveScrews through a thin back wherever studs seem to be: The load path and edge distances are unknown. Do not hang a loaded cabinet from an unverified skin or guessed framing.

Decision challenge 2 of 3

How will the cabinet meet a sloped ceiling or wall?

A square cabinet needs a controlled reveal or fit part where the room is irregular.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathKeep the box square and fit a separate scribe or crown zone: Room variation stays outside the door and shelf geometry. Use trim to meet the room while the cabinet remains true.

Conditional — verifyUse an oversized finished end at one exposed side: A single end can absorb variation if door clearance and grain are preserved. Model the trimming limit and exposed reveal before cutting.

Stop and resolveRack the cabinet until both corners touch: Doors, shelves, and backs lose their intended geometry. Never use cabinet distortion as a fitting method.

Decision challenge 3 of 3

When should doors be installed?

Doors help verify reveals but add weight and can obstruct safe lifting and fastening.

Select the verified project condition

Carry the choice into the released plan

Strong pathFit and label doors on the bench, then remove them for hanging: Hinge geometry is proven while installation weight stays manageable. Preserve hinge positions and reinstall after the boxes are aligned.

Conditional — verifyInstall doors only after the boxes are hung: This can work when a separate sample proved the hinge and drilling setup. Do not discover a drilling-pattern error across an installed run.

Stop and resolveLift fully loaded cabinets with doors and shelves installed: Weight and uncontrolled movement increase installation risk. Remove loose and heavy components before lifting.

After each reveal, record the verified condition in the brief and update every dependent dimension, material row, phase, and release check before continuing.

Six-phase path

Move from verified input to documented finish

Complete phases in the order shown unless the released project plan defines a safer dependency. Each checkbox represents a reviewed phase, not merely activity. Preserve the input, observed result, owner, date, revision, and exception record that lets another person reproduce why the work moved forward.

01

Phase 1 of 6 · 60–90 min

Measure

Outcome

Map the wall, attachment structure, vertical datums, and every swing or service conflict.

Work sequence

  • Survey wall plumb and ceiling variation.
  • Verify framing or masonry attachment zones.
  • Trace door, appliance, light, and reach envelopes.
Evidence to keep

Wall elevation with structure, no-drill zones, and controlling heights.

Hold point

Stop if the intended fastener locations or concealed services are unknown.

02

Phase 2 of 6 · 2–3 hr

Plan

Outcome

Resolve box grouping, hanging system, shelf loads, doors, fillers, and installation order.

Work sequence

  • Size boxes around the verified run.
  • Design rails or structural backs.
  • Calculate doors, reveals, and end conditions.
Evidence to keep

Cabinet schedule and wall-attachment drawing with load-path notes.

Hold point

Stop if the cabinet-to-wall load path cannot be described component by component.

03

Phase 3 of 6 · 3–4 hr

Cut

Outcome

Cut matched cabinet parts and machine repeatable attachment and hinge references.

Work sequence

  • Break down sides with grain and inside faces labeled.
  • Cut rails or backs from stable stock.
  • Bore shelf and hinge patterns on a verified handed sample.
Evidence to keep

Labeled parts plus passing hinge-pattern and rail-fit samples.

Hold point

Stop when left/right parts, faces, or top/bottom references are ambiguous.

04

Phase 4 of 6 · 3–4 hr

Assemble

Outcome

Build square boxes and prove the back or rail connection before installation.

Work sequence

  • Dry-fit box, back, and hanging members.
  • Square from diagonals before fixing the back.
  • Load-check shelf supports and fit doors on the bench.
Evidence to keep

Square cabinet with recorded diagonals and verified hanging components.

Hold point

Stop if the back or rail can move independently of the box under hand load.

05

Phase 5 of 6 · 2–4 hr

Install

Outcome

Transfer the cabinet load safely into verified structure while keeping the run level and aligned.

Work sequence

  • Set a temporary support or approved hanging rail.
  • Lift stripped boxes and secure initial fasteners.
  • Shim, join, and final-fasten without racking the cases.
Evidence to keep

Level and plumb run with fastener locations and cabinet joints recorded.

Hold point

Stop if a fastener misses structure or a service zone conflicts with the plan.

06

Phase 6 of 6 · 45–75 min

Closeout

Outcome

Restore doors and shelves, verify function and load assumptions, and document the attachment system.

Work sequence

  • Adjust paired doors and confirm full swing.
  • Load shelves gradually within the design boundary.
  • Record fasteners, stud locations, fillers, and touchups.
Evidence to keep

Function test, attachment photos, and final reveal record.

Hold point

Do not close if a door collision, cabinet movement, or unresolved fastener remains.

Adapt without losing control

Useful variations and what they force you to revisit

A variation is not a cosmetic label. It can change the finished envelope, part set, sheet grouping, joinery, hardware, load, stability, transport, finish, installation, or removal path. Choose it early enough to update the authoritative project sources.

Finished-interior open cabinet

Use when: Use when the cabinet has no doors and every inside face, edge, and hanging detail remains visible.

Carry through: Material face selection, seam location, fastener concealment, and shelf-edge finish become primary layout inputs.

Glass-door display cabinet

Use when: Use after glass, hinge, shelf, and displayed-object weights and safety boundaries are confirmed.

Carry through: Door sag, shelf load, reflected reveals, and lighting clearances require tighter bench and installed function checks.

Continuous hanging-rail garage run

Use when: Use where verified wall structure supports a level rated rail across multiple cabinet bays.

Carry through: Individual boxes share one attachment datum, so rail straightness, connector access, and end restraint control the installation.

Catch rework while it is still a decision

Project risks, early signals, and hold actions

Use these risks as observation prompts rather than predictions. When a signal appears, protect the current condition, keep affected work visible, identify the smallest upstream cause, and repeat every dependent calculation, sample, fit, or inspection under the corrected revision.

Concealed-service strike

Early signal: A planned wall fastener enters an unverified or marked no-drill zone.

Response: Stop drilling, confirm service location with an appropriate method, and redesign the attachment layout rather than shifting blindly.

Back-panel load shortcut

Early signal: Wall screws bear only on a thin back skin or the back moves relative to the case.

Response: Unload the cabinet and add or redesign a verified structural rail/back connection tied into the case sides.

Room variation racks the case

Early signal: Diagonals and door reveals change when the cabinet is drawn toward the wall or ceiling.

Response: Keep the case square, correct shims and fit parts outside the box, and repeat the attachment check.

Door swing collision

Early signal: A door contacts crown, side wall, appliance, lighting, or its neighboring door before full travel.

Response: Hold final adjustment and revise hinge angle, filler, door size, or adjacent condition from the measured collision envelope.

Never solve a mismatch by trimming or forcing the downstream part without recording what changed. A local adjustment can hide a wrong datum, wrong product, wrong material thickness, unsafe clearance, blocked assembly sequence, or installation conflict that will reappear later.

Finish line

Close the project against observable results

The wall cabinet transfers its load through documented cabinet members and fasteners into verified structure while the case remains square and usable.

  • The cabinet top and bottom datums remain level after joining and final fastening.
  • Recorded fasteners land in the verified structure and the back or hanging rail does not move relative to the case.
  • Every door opens to its released angle without contact and paired reveals remain consistent.
  • Shelves remain seated and the cabinet shows no movement under the declared staged load.

Closeout should leave a usable trail for repair, repeat work, disassembly, warranty questions, and future changes. Save the released drawing or sketch, final parts and material records, product identities, approved exceptions, field changes, finish information, photographs where appropriate, and the final project-playbook CSV together.

Safety and authority boundaries

Use the playbook to expose uncertainty, not to override it

Follow current manufacturer instructions and use appropriate training, guarding, support, dust or fume control, ventilation, PPE, lifting methods, cure time, and disposal practices. Do not use this page as permission to operate unfamiliar equipment, defeat a safety system, exceed a tool or product rating, improvise a structural connection, or conceal work that has not passed the required inspection.

Structural, stair, guard, anchoring, seismic, electrical, plumbing, gas, fire, accessibility, and building-code decisions require the appropriate qualified professional or authority. Hardware clearances, fastener schedules, adhesive preparation, coating compatibility, load ratings, and installation methods come from the exact current product and project documentation. Verify them after any substitution.

Dimensions must name their datum, unit, finished or unfinished state, and measurement source. Check real openings at multiple locations; confirm square, level, plumb, flatness, obstructions, access, transport, assembly, service, and removal paths. Build a first article, sample, mockup, or dry fit when one failure could repeat across a batch or become expensive after finishing or installation.

Questions before starting

Project playbook FAQ

Can I build the wall cabinet project playbook exactly as shown?

No. This page is a planning playbook rather than a dimensioned drawing or approval. Verify the real site, design, loads, material, hardware, joinery, tooling, finish, installation, and applicable requirements before irreversible work.

Where is my project progress saved?

When browser storage is available, progress stays locally in this browser under a versioned WoodCutTool record. This page does not require an account and does not send the brief, choices, or phase status to a server.

What should I do when a phase cannot be completed?

Keep the affected work on hold. Identify whether the mismatch comes from the design, measurement, material, hardware, setup, site, sequence, or current instructions. Correct the controlling source and repeat dependent checks before continuing.

Which CSV should I download?

The static project-playbook.csv is a blank durable record of the published brief, materials, decisions, and six phases. The progress CSV is created in your browser and includes the values and completion state currently entered on the page.
