SHELF PIN STUDIO / FIELD GUIDE 04

Shelf Pin Keep-Outs Around Hinges, Backs and Joints

A hole can fit inside the panel and still conflict with a hinge plate, back groove, fixing or support body. Keep-outs make the interruption visible before the jig reaches that part of the side.

By WoodCutTool editorial team · Reviewed 2026-10-15

Open your hole map ↗

WORKED REVIEW

A band from 300 to 360 mm suppresses the sample centers at 320 and 352 mm. A 5 mm hole centered at 256 mm also conflicts with a band starting at 258.5 mm: its rim touches the boundary. The tool omits that entire four-hole level on both panels.

1. Measure the full occupied region2. Use a conservative full-width band3. Include the hole radius at the boundary4. Keep the original grid through the interruption5. Review the shelf and door together

STEP 01

Measure the full occupied region

Start with the actual hardware and cabinet joint drawings, then compare them with a physical mock-up wherever possible. Include the body of a support, a hinge plate, fastener paths, a back groove and the swept space required to operate or service the hardware. A visible screw head alone is not the whole envelope. Measure the lowest and highest affected heights from the same finished bottom datum as the pin grid. Add whatever project-specific separation your hardware and construction require before recording the exclusion; the planner does not invent that clearance.

STEP 02

Use a conservative full-width band

Each entered keep-out extends across the entire depth of both panels. This deliberately omits front and rear holes at the affected heights, even if the physical obstacle occurs near only one row or one side. It keeps the remaining levels complete: every available height has a left-front, left-rear, right-front and right-rear hole. That choice may remove more adjustment positions than a custom engineered pattern would. The tool does not offer partial support sets or claim that three supports can replace four. If the project needs asymmetric rows, prepare a separate reviewed layout.

STEP 03

Include the hole radius at the boundary

The overlap test compares the complete vertical extent of each circular hole with each entered band. A center is blocked when center + radius reaches the band bottom and center − radius has not passed the band top. Boundary contact counts as a conflict. This geometry is useful when a center looks just outside a colored strip on a small screen. It still tests only a hole circle against your recorded band; it does not know the size of a pin shoulder, shelf notch or moving hinge. Include those additional envelopes when measuring the band.

STEP 04

Keep the original grid through the interruption

The first allowed center above a band is the next existing grid position that clears it. The tool never shifts the upper row segment to make a new first hole at the band edge. Preserve the same behavior in the shop: mark omitted jig bushings, bridge the gap with a verified reference or use the jig maker’s appropriate continuation method. A gap is not a new origin. Check the first and last centers after the interruption against the bottom datum before drilling the remaining side, especially when the jig has been unclamped.

STEP 05

Review the shelf and door together

Select an available level and install the actual support and shelf during the test assembly. Cycle the nearby door and inspect both the shelf edge and support bodies through the intended travel. Check rear engagement with the back installed and allow for whatever removal access the project needs. A successful circle-and-band screen does not evaluate moving clearances, bearing area or fastener capacity. Record the measured envelope and any added allowance in the notes so a later hardware substitution prompts a new review instead of inheriting an unexplained colored band.

Before continuing

Stop if a hardware change invalidates the recorded envelope or a remaining level lacks a complete tested support set.

Can I block only the front row?

This planner uses full-width bands on both sides so each remaining level has four corresponding holes. An asymmetric support plan needs a separate design review.

References and model boundary

The manufacturer references describe their own jig systems. Project dimensions, grid phase, keep-out envelopes and remaining-skin requirements belong to your measured setup. The planner tests its stated geometry; use the actual hardware instructions and a physical test pair before drilling production panels.
