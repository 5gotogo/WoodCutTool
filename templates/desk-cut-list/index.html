Desk Cut List Template

Use this desk cut list template to plan a compact plywood desk before cutting. It starts with a 48 by 24 inch top, two side panels, simple stretchers, and a lower shelf. Adjust the height, width, and storage to fit your room, then test the exact panel layout with your real plywood sheet size and saw kerf.

A practical plywood desk layout

A straightforward desk is a good first sheet-goods project because its parts are mostly rectangles: a top, two full-height sides, a lower shelf, and narrow stretchers that keep the frame square. This version is designed around a 29-1/4 inch finished height and a 48 inch working width. The dimensions leave room for a standard chair while keeping the large pieces simple to break down from a 4x8 sheet.

Use 3/4 inch plywood for the visible structure. If you want a lighter desk or a painted finish, choose a panel material that suits that use and check the sheet goods material library before buying. The template is a starting point, not a structural rating: add wall anchoring, thicker material, or bracing when the desk must carry unusually heavy equipment.

The side panels sit under the top, which creates the target finished height after the 3/4 inch desktop is added. The shelf is set back from the front so there is toe room, and the stretchers connect the sides while keeping the desk from racking. If you need drawers, treat them as a separate subassembly and begin with the drawer box cut list template rather than guessing at clearances.

Plan the sheet layout before buying plywood

The 24 by 48 inch desktop is the largest rectangle, so place it first in a layout. The two side panels and lower shelf can then be nested around the remaining area. Do not estimate the sheet count from square footage alone: the remaining space may have the wrong shape, and every saw cut removes material. Enter the parts in the plywood cut calculator with the actual blade kerf and any grain-direction rules to confirm the plan.

For a desk that may change as you work, save the project in the CutList optimizer app. It is especially useful when you are comparing a wider top, a second shelf, or a drawer bay, because you can see whether a small design change forces another sheet.

Cut order and assembly checks

Break down the full plywood sheet into manageable strips first, then crosscut the desktop and large panels. Cut matching side panels as a pair and label their inside faces before assembly. A shallow groove or cleat can support the lower shelf; pocket screws, confirmat screws, or glue-and-screw joinery can work when chosen for the material. Use the screw size finder to choose a practical starting point, and test the fastener in an offcut before drilling the visible parts.

Before final assembly, dry-fit the sides, shelf, and stretchers on a flat surface. Check both diagonal measurements to confirm the frame is square, then attach the top after the base is stable. For a more detailed planning workflow, read how to read a cut list and saw kerf explained.
