Refrigerator Surround Cabinet Cut List Template

A refrigerator enclosure with tall side panels, bridge cabinet, ventilation and door-swing clearance, and a removable service path. Use this as a starting point, then change every dimension for your room, hardware, material thickness, and joinery before cutting.

Release boundary for this template

Do not release these plywood parts from nominal dimensions alone. Close the measurement, system, and installation decisions first.

  • Field measurement: Measure the finished appliance bay at floor, counter, and top heights after flooring and wall finishes are known.
  • Hardware and service system: Approved refrigerator data controls clear opening, air path, hinge clearance, service route, and bridge height.
  • Installation boundary: Side panels and fillers are scribed only after the appliance bay is level, square, and checked for removal access.

Use Field-measure the appliance enclosure to prepare the input, and Diagnose an appliance bay that is too small if the drawing, hardware, or installed condition no longer agrees.

Plan the refrigerator surround cabinet before buying sheets

Start by confirming the finished width, depth, height, access clearances, and load requirements. This template is intentionally made from rectangular plywood parts so you can adapt it without redrawing the whole project. Keep matching pieces grouped together, mark visible faces, and leave room for edge banding, backs, doors, drawers, hinges, or trim that apply to your version.

Use 3/4 inch plywood for the structural parts unless your design calls for another thickness. A template is not a structural rating: add bracing, wall anchoring, weather protection, or a thicker panel whenever the location, load, or local requirements demand it.

Refrigerator Surround Cabinet project-specific checks

Use these checks with the sample parts list before calculating sheet count or making a purchase.

  • Use the appliance guide for ventilation, hinge-side clearance, door opening angle, handle projection, water line, outlet, and leveling access.
  • Confirm the refrigerator can be removed without dismantling fixed countertop, crown, flooring transitions, or adjacent cabinet faces.
  • Size the bridge cabinet and structural support independently from decorative side panels and refrigerator top trim.
  • Keep field-sized fillers available for wall bow, appliance centering, and neighboring handle clearance.

Check the sheet layout and cut order

Place the largest parts first, then nest matching rectangles around them. Square footage alone cannot tell you the sheet count because saw kerf, grain direction, and the shape of the remaining offcuts matter. Enter this adjusted list in the plywood cut calculator with your real sheet size and blade kerf before purchasing material.

Break full sheets into manageable strips first, then crosscut the final parts. Keep pairs together, label the inside faces, and dry-fit the case on a flat surface before driving final fasteners. The cut list calculator is useful if the project also uses solid-wood rails, trim, or stretchers.

Assembly checks that prevent rework

Dry-fit the major panels, measure both diagonals for square, and confirm that moving parts or equipment have the clearance you planned. Pre-drill near plywood edges, test fasteners in an offcut, and attach the back only after the cabinet or frame is square. Read how to read a cut list and saw kerf explained if you need a refresher before finalizing the layout.

How this template was prepared

The parts list models a common refrigerator surround cabinet carcass or frame so you have named pieces and repeated quantities to review. It deliberately leaves hardware, joinery, edge treatment, structural loads, and site conditions open because those choices change final dimensions. Before cutting, verify every opening and finished dimension, confirm material thickness with a caliper, apply hardware clearances, and review the optimized layout against the drawing or room.
